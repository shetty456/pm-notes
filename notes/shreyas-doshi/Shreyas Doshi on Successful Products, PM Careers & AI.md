## 🚫 Why Well-Funded Companies Still Launch Failed Products

### The Core Misconception: Shipping ≠ Success
> *"I have never once met a team that was never able to ship the product. And yet most of the energy that is spent is around shipping the product."*

- Hundreds of thousands of products ship every year
- Very few succeed
- Therefore: shipping is necessary but clearly not sufficient
- Most product content online is "highly confusing" — "all that matters is shipping" gets 1,000 likes but is demonstrably false

### Problem 1: Learning and Applying the Wrong Things

**The wrong question pattern:**
- People ask: "How did you solve it at Stripe?" or "What made you successful at Snapchat?"
- These are the wrong questions — the premise is flawed
- *"It does not matter how we solved it at Stripe"*
- American culture compounds this: people ask "what would you do?" instead of "what should I do?"

**The garbage cycle:**
1. Ask the wrong question (how did famous company X solve this?)
2. Get a sincere but context-specific answer (mostly garbage for your situation)
3. Present it to your team as gold
4. Team gets excited (authority bias + novelty)
5. Implement the garbage
6. Repeat

> *"I asked the wrong question, the person gave me some answer which was sincere but there's a bunch of garbage in there. You can't separate the real signal from the garbage. You took what was mostly garbage, framed it as gold, and the team implemented it."*

### Problem 2: The Real Objective Function in Most Companies

> *"In most companies, the goal isn't even success. The real objective function is to create a certain perception of progress, a certain perception of competence, a certain perception of having everything under control."*

**What actually happens in 9 out of 10 product reviews:**
- What SHOULD happen: share insights about market, customers, competition → translate into a compelling, differentiated product
- What DOES happen:
  - Show we have it under control
  - Present fancy reports with math nobody understands (emperor has no clothes)
  - Present a buttoned-up plan with false certainty
  - Ask for 3 more engineers and cross-functional alignment

**Why this happens at every level:**
- Creative PM → reports to GPM/Director → VP → SVP → CEO
- For a highly creative product to manifest, EVERY layer must be:
  - Highly creative and entrepreneurial
  - Desire for impact first, not optics
  - Not worried about next promotion or team size requirements
- *"People at different levels are thinking about all this junk when they are trying to make a decision about what product proposal to make"*

**The result:**
- Highly creative, differentiated, ambitious ideas get watered down to something safe
- Something that can be justified with data science numbers
- Something that gets approval and headcount
- *"The idea of actually launching a highly resonant creative differentiated product takes a back seat"*

**This is rampant even at world-class organizations as they scale** — not incompetent companies, but highly competent companies where this behavior spreads as they grow.

## 💡 How to Actually Build Successful Products (If You're Impact-Focused)

### Step 1: Understand the 3X Framework (Kent Beck)

Three stages every product goes through:

| Stage | Description | Key Metric Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Explore** | Zero to one, early stage | Qualitative, insight-focused |
| **Expand** | Product-market fit, hyper growth | 2-3 core metrics (revenue, growth rate, market share) |
| **Extract** | Mature, slower growth on large denominator | Move metric A from value X to value Y |

**The OKR mistake (the "Bob" problem):**
- Bob insists every KR must be "move metric from value X to value Y"
- This is correct for Extract stage (Facebook news feed, Uber core product)
- This is **wrong for Explore stage**
- Applying Extract-stage OKRs to Explore-stage products all but guarantees failure

**Why quantitative OKRs kill Explore-stage products:**
- If you have "get 5 customers to sign LOI" as a KR
- Two weeks before quarter end: you're in panic mode, not insight mode
- You'll promise customers anything to hit the number
- You'll do it "under the guise of being user-centric" but the objective function is defense, not winning

> *"The chief goal of explore stage is to resolve ambiguities — about what customers need, what solution will work, how the market will receive it, what they'll pay. Getting 5 customers to sign an LOI is actually a distraction."*

**What Explore-stage OKRs should look like:**
- Qualitative is fine
- Shipping something is fine
- Insight-focused goals are fine
- NOT: quantitative metrics you don't yet have data to set

**The systemic problem:**
- Large companies create standard processes for every launch (for consistency and fairness)
- They apply Extract-stage processes to Explore-stage products
- This kills products before they have a chance to live

### Step 2: Have Courage

> *"Innovation is not about a dearth of ideas. Innovation is actually about a lack of courage."*

- If you're constantly in defense mode ("I hope I don't get fired, I hope executives are happy")
- You will make these mistakes over and over
- Being entrepreneurial = being willing to break some rules

### Step 3: Fixate on User, Market, and Competition

- Understand customers deeply
- Understand the market deeply
- Understand competition very well
- Then: come up with specific creative ideas for how to translate insight into product

**The missing step most people skip:**

Most people: Customer insight → Product features (directly)
Problem: Your competition is doing the same thing → no differentiation

What you should do: Customer insight → **[Add high degree of creativity]** → Product proposal

### Step 4: Honestly Assess Your Own Creativity

> *"The degree of creativity required to make consistently winning products is very high — both at macro level (what features) and micro level (creative execution). You need to be top 10% in product creativity."*

- Most people cannot admit they're not top 10% in creativity
- "I'm a PM, I'm supposed to be the one making decisions about what to build, therefore I must be creative"
- This is a stumbling block at all levels, including CPO

**What great product leaders do instead:**
- High degree of self-awareness about their own creativity level
- Outcome-focused, not title-focused
- Find the most creative person on the team (could be a junior engineer, designer, anyone)
- Partner with them: "Here's what competition does, here's what I heard from customers — what do you think?"

> *"I guarantee you there is at least one person on a team of 10+ people at an above-average talent company who is a creative product person. But you are not leveraging their insights."*

### Step 5: Master Influence

- Once you have a differentiated proposal, you need to get it done
- Influence with cross-functional peers, other teams, executives
- Find out what triggers executives, what their work priorities are
- Shape your proposal to align with that
- If impossible to align: do the hard work of convincing them

> *"The CEO of your company wants exactly all of this to happen. Problem is there's a lot of noise in the middle layers where people are concerned about a lot more than just successful products."*

## 📊 The LNO Framework

### Origin
- Evolved from Elizabeth Grace Saunders' IRO technique
- Created during a period of extreme stress at Google: endless task list, affecting personal life, sleep, quality of work

### The Three Task Types

| Type | Definition | How to Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **L (Leverage)** | 10x, 100x, 100,000x return on effort | Take as much time as needed; schedule during most productive time (e.g., early morning) |
| **N (Neutral)** | Equal return — put in X, get X back | Aim for a strictly okay job (your "okay" is still pretty good due to quality drive) |
| **O (Overhead)** | Negative return — put in X, get 0.1X back | Actively try to do a bad job; just meet minimum criteria |

**L task example:** Writing the AI strategy for your product area — could generate 10x, 100x, or 100,000x returns

**N task example:** Writing a PRD for a non-core feature — needs to be done, but not the main thing

**O task example:** Filing expense reports — just fill it in, don't agonize over the description

### Why LNO Beats the Eisenhower Matrix
- Eisenhower Matrix (urgent/important) is great but leaves you with too many tasks in the "do" categories
- Even after Eisenhower, you're still overwhelmed
- LNO tells you **how much effort to put into each remaining task**
- Practical tip: prefix each task in your to-do list with L, N, or O

### The Key Insight
> *"As perfectionists, we often don't understand how much effort needs to go into a given task. We allocate the same amount of effort and attention to every task."*

**The satisfaction benefit:**
- After implementing LNO, Shreyas drove home much more satisfied
- Even with many tasks remaining, knowing he tackled the high-leverage ones = satisfaction
- *"The more important aspect of the LNO system is it gives you a much higher degree of satisfaction with your work"*

## 🎯 How to Measure Creativity (When You Can't Measure It Directly)

### The Core Principle
> *"Creativity cannot be measured, but creativity can be evaluated — on a relative basis."*

- There is no metric for creativity
- But you can evaluate it relative to other products, other people, other solutions

### For Early-Career PMs (< 5-6 years)
- Ask your manager: "What are my superpowers?"
- This question will catch most managers off guard (it's their job to know this proactively, but most don't)
- Tactical approach:
  1. Ask the question
  2. Say: "I don't need an answer right now — let's discuss in our next 1:1"
  3. Ask them to mull it over
  4. Extract patterns from the examples they give you (they'll give examples, not patterns)

**The Steph Curry principle:**
> *"Steph Curry doesn't say 'today I choose to shoot the basketball well.' That's not how talent works. Jerry Seinfeld doesn't say 'today I choose to be funny.' He can't help it. He sees hilarity everywhere."*

- If you're highly creative, it would have manifested in many ways even in a non-creative environment
- Don't use "my organization hasn't given me the opportunity to be creative" as an excuse

### For Mid-Career PMs (5+ years)
- You now have enough sample size to evaluate yourself relatively
- You've done many product launches, worked with many teams, gone through hundreds of sprints
- Compare your products to other products
- Compare your solutions to what others would have come up with

**Shreyas's personal example:**
- Realized he had very high cognitive empathy for customers — but nobody told him for the first 5-6 years
- Only figured it out when he started mentoring and managing other PMs
- *"Early in our careers we assume everybody is like us. Only later we realize some people don't have what I seem to have, and others have a lot of what I don't have."*

## 🚀 First 90 Days on the Job

### The Right Lens
- Don't think about tactics to create a good impression
- Think about: what habits does a PM need throughout their time on a product? Create conditions for those habits to be established.
- *"If you take it through this lens, you will actually end up creating a much better impression"*

### Concrete Recommendations

**First 30 days: Use your own product deeply**
- No course, book, YouTube video, or Twitter thread substitutes for using your own product
- Your peers will notice — they'll see you asking questions, making remarks about confusing parts
- This creates a very positive impression organically

**First 30 days: Build relationships the right way**
- Do 1:1s with key team members — but focus on it being the FIRST STEP of a highly productive relationship
- Ask about: their background, what excites them about their work, what frustrates them
- Don't: immediately assign tasks or start interrogating about sprint status
- *"The person walked away from this introductory 1:1 with a bunch of tasks to do. That is not a good first impression."*

**Ask your manager: "Who are the 5 best people you know at this company?"**
- Go meet them — regardless of whether they work on your product
- Purpose: understand what "great" looks like at this company
- You'll see common patterns across these people
- *"None of your peers have this signal because they're busy running SQL queries to show they can do three-table joins"*

**Other first 90-day activities:**
- Observe a customer meeting
- Observe a sales meeting
- Get ramped up on the interview process

## 🤖 How PMs Should Approach AI

### Category 1: How Will AI Impact Your Domain/Product?

**If AI will disrupt your category entirely** (e.g., legal research software, customer support software):
- Be very vigilant and proactive
- Even if it's not your "official" responsibility, offer proposals and suggestions

**If AI is a differentiator but not a disruptor:**
- Think about how to enhance your product using what's now possible with AI

**The "How Will AI Impact X?" Exercise:**
- Get together with PMs, engineers, designers on a Friday
- Pick any domain: podcasts, real estate purchases, parenting, coaching, higher education, horticulture
- Ask: how will AI impact this domain?
- Purpose: exercise those creative pathways in your brain
- *"Creativity is a result of various actions you take or don't take. You can't plan for creativity but you can do these types of things that will make you a lot more creative in the future."*

### Category 2: How Will AI Impact Teams?

**Prediction: Teams will get much smaller**
- Less need for the project management heavy-lifting PMs currently do
- Analytical insights: instead of half a day of data analysis, just ask AI
- Market research: AI saves 80% of time
- UI/UX: declarative creation of UI markups
- Code: declarative code creation

**The "Builder" prediction:**
> *"We might end up in a scenario where in the product team of the future, there are maybe five or six people doing the same output as a 20, 30, or even 50-person team. And the titles of those five people might just be the same — 'Builder' or 'Maker' or 'Product Builder.'"*

- Engineers doing market research
- Engineers creating decent first versions of UX/UI
- Titles may blur or disappear

### Category 3: Impact on Your Career

**The core skills that become MORE important with AI:**
- Customer insight
- Market insight
- Creativity
- Creative execution
- Influence

**Don't avoid AI out of fear:**
> *"Ignoring what's happening with AI is akin to ignoring what was happening in the 90s with the World Wide Web."*

- It's okay to have avoided Web3, crypto, even the mobile wave
- AI is different — don't ignore it
- First step: recognize the fear exists, accept it, then engage

## 🔄 Advice for Those Wanting to Transition to Coaching/Advising/Teaching

### Question 1: Why Are You Doing It?

> *"Is there love? Are you doing it because you love it?"*

**The power law problem:**
- Above-average PM = decent income, comfortable life
- Above-average coach = nearly zero dollars
- The power law is very unforgiving in coaching/teaching/advising
- Top 10% coach: decent income
- Top 5% coach: really good income
- Top 1% coach: more than PM leadership role + more flexibility

**The practical problem if you don't love it:**
- Without love, you cannot sustain the commitment needed to reach top 10%, 5%, 1%
- Self-discipline alone won't get you there

### Question 2: Are You Willing to Commit to Becoming Top 10%?

**The wrong questions people ask:**
- "How do you monetize this?"
- "How do you price this?"
- "How do you market this?"

**The right question:**
> *"How do I become an amazing teacher/coach/advisor for PMs?"*

- Marketing tactics and pricing tactics are useless if you're not top 10%
- Great marketing might get first signups, but repeat business requires genuine excellence
- *"Focus on competence first. In the next 1-2-3 years, just fixate on that competence before you start this next chapter."*
