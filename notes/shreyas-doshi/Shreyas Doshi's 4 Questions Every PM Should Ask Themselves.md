## ❓ Question 1: Why Am I So Busy?

### The Problem
- Shreyas spent ~16-17 years being completely stressed out, dissatisfied at the end of every day
- Physical manifestations: back muscle pulls, aches and pains that were actually stress-related
- *"Even though I was doing a good job, I was not feeling very good inside"*

### The Productivity Trap
- All the standard advice works... until it doesn't:
  - LNO framework ✓
  - Calendar management ✓
  - To-do lists ✓
  - Working out for energy ✓
- The immovable force: **scope**

> *"At some point, your scope will be so large that no matter what efficiency framework you use, you are still going to be incredibly busy."*

### The Annual Planning Trap (A Case Study)
**The cycle:**
1. Spend 4-6 weeks on planning (spreadsheets, meetings, dependencies, stakeholders)
2. Present to executives → get asked "what if money were no concern?" / "what if we gave you 5 more engineers?"
3. Publish the plan with enthusiasm
4. January: 3 customer escalations for features NOT in the plan
5. Revise resources, talk to dependency teams, revise plan
6. By late February: everyone's forgotten the actual plan
7. Someone quotes Eisenhower: *"Plans are useless, but planning is everything"* — nobody knows what it means, but everyone nods

**The Stripe Revelation:**
- Shreyas had built a **real product strategy** for Stripe Connect earlier in the year
- When planning season came: his planning took **3 days** instead of 4-6 weeks
- Peers were still in their multi-week planning cycles
- Key move: refused to fill out templates that didn't make sense
- *"If a template doesn't make sense, why should I fill it out?"*

### The Two-Way Door Trap (The Real Reason You're Busy)

**The scenario:**
1. Team has a meeting about a feature request
2. Someone invokes Bezos: "This is a two-way door, just make a decision quickly"
3. Team commits to the feature (5-6 weeks to build + ramp-up)
4. Feature launches with low adoption
5. At the QBR: CEO asks how it's doing
6. PM uses favorable anecdotes: *"This one customer said it was life-changing"* (even if they're the only user)
7. Sales says: "We're still not winning deals because of this feature"
8. Someone says: "It's not full-featured yet, we need to meet table stakes"
9. PM is now committed to even MORE work for a feature that shouldn't have been built

> *"Through a product leader's life, what happens is we just accumulate all of this debt, feature after feature. That's why we're busy."*

**The key insight:**
> *"Most doors that look like two-way doors are actually one-way doors. They are two-way doors at Bezos' level, but as a PM leader, for you, they are a one-way door."*

### The Two Tactical Tips

**1. Have a real product strategy**
- Not a fake one — a real, cohesive strategy with genuine alignment
- This makes planning a breeze and gives you a rigorous framework for handling escalations
- *"If you have a real product strategy, a lot of this nonsense we tend to do with annual planning actually goes away"*

**2. Pause before making product decisions**
- Pause for 2 minutes, 2 days, or 2 weeks before committing
- Think through: customer motivation, differentiation, distribution approach
- Most product decisions happen based on "Is Bob the engineer free?" — not on actual product thinking
- *"Thinking is cheap, so you should do more thinking, not less"*

## ❓ Question 2: Do I Actually Have Good Taste?

### The Background: The Google Mistake
- At Google (early in career, <5 years in product): heard repeatedly that "strategy is for MBAs, we're all about execution"
- Naively repeated this mantra for 6 years
- A wiser peer kept asking "Shreyas, what is your strategy here?" — Shreyas dismissed him

### The Twitter Revelation (circa 2014)
- Twitter post-IPO had incredible assets: product, network effects, brand, talent
- Yet the product was struggling, not meeting its potential
- After 6-9 months: *"Twitter's biggest problem is a product strategy problem"*
- This shattered the "execution is everything, strategy is useless" belief

### What Taste Actually Means

> *"Taste is about the ability to identify what is really good, without needing to see its results."*

**The Jensen Huang example:**
- Saying Jensen Huang is a genius in 2024 requires **zero taste** — just look at the stock price
- Saying it in 2010 requires real taste
- Jensen didn't change much between 2010 and 2024 — the world just caught up

**"Game recognize game" — but before the game is called**
- It requires no genius to say Patrick Mahomes is a great quarterback today
- Real taste = recognizing greatness in the practice session, not after the championship

### The Three Patterns That Reveal Poor Taste

**1. Getting overly excited about catchy metaphors**
- "Two-way door / one-way door" vs. "reversible / irreversible decisions" — same concept
- The catchy metaphor wins because of the metaphor itself + authority bias (Bezos said it)

**2. Getting impressed by alliterations**
- "Fail fast" — would you be as attracted to "fail quickly"? Same meaning, no alliteration
- "Fast follow" — same thing
- *"The only thing that changed is one is an alliteration"*

**3. Getting impressed by complicated charts and math you don't understand**
- Some senior product leaders actually use this as a strategy

### The Real Insight About Taste

> *"Do I have good taste around the beliefs I choose to create within myself as a product leader? Those beliefs end up dictating everything — how I manage, how I lead, how I make decisions."*

- It's not just about beautiful pixels and perfect product design
- It's about evaluating ideas **separate from their social proof, authority proof, and whatever else**
- Shedding these patterns = becoming a more genuine critical thinker
- *"We all think we are critical thinkers, but we are not"*

**The tough grader trap:**
- As we get more senior and successful, we become reflexive critics: "This is crap, this is crap, this is crap"
- That also requires zero taste — anyone can say everything is horrible

## ❓ Question 3: Why Does My Job Feel So Frustrating?

### The Paradox
- Shreyas loved his PM leadership job — wouldn't exchange it for anything
- Yet: daily frustrations, despite loving the macro

### The Root Cause
> *"Our jobs get frustrating when we behave, most of the time, in misalignment with our superpowers and who we truly are at our core."*

### The Three Levels of Product Work Framework
(Referenced from his earlier podcast episode)

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| **Impact** | Focused on outcomes, strategy, what matters |
| **Execution** | Focused on getting things done, shipping |
| **Optics** | Focused on how things look, stakeholder management, visibility |

**The key insight:**
- Every PM leader has a **preferred level** (their "happy place")
- Shreyas's happy place: the **impact level**
- Many PM leaders' happy place: the **execution level**
- The problem: as you go higher in the corporate ladder, you are forced to spend more and more time at the **optics level**

### The Stripe Decision
- When his team grew to ~50 people (including engineers), Shreyas recognized:
  - Law of corporations: teams of 50, 100, and beyond = forced to spend significant time at the optics level
  - This was fundamentally misaligned with who he is
- His solution: went back to an earlier-stage product
- *"I'm not going to just play the corporate game"*

### The Tactical Advice

> *"To thine own self be true." — Shakespeare*

1. **Identify your superpowers** — be honest about what level you naturally operate at
2. **Make career decisions based on your superpowers**, not on:
   - External expectations
   - LinkedIn envy ("this person from my grad school is at a different level")
   - What your mom expects
   - What your LinkedIn profile progression looks like

> *"If you identify your superpowers and work in accordance with them, you will do the best work of your life. You will love it, and you will be great at it, and you won't have that frustration."*

## ❓ Question 4 (Bonus): Am I Really Listening?

### The False Confidence Trap
- Shreyas thought he was a good listener because he:
  - Listened attentively
  - Recapped what he heard
  - Made eye contact
  - Said "this is what I heard"
- *"All of that nonsense"* — these are surface-level listening behaviors

### The Real Insight
> *"There is an entirely other level to listening, which once you understand it, is what enables you to be a world-class leader."*
