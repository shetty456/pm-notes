## 🚨 The Problem: Feedback for PMs is Broken

### What Typical PM Feedback Looks Like
- Once every 3–6 months, manager recaps deliverables and 360 feedback
- Common feedback examples:
  - "Give engineers more rationale when priorities change"
  - "Give designers more heads-up for user research"
  - "Think more strategically, less tactically"

### Why It Fails
- Feedback is **process-driven**, not tailored to what the PM actually needs
- Vague directives like "be more strategic" are never explained — *what does that mean? how do I do it?*
- Not all feedback is even valid for a given PM's situation
- PM managers are busy — it's not their fault, it's a structural gap

## 🧠 The LMS Framework

> *"The way you should be thinking about your career as a product manager is in terms of the LMS framework."*

| Letter | Stands For | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **L** | Liabilities | Things you're poor at that hurt your team, product, or career |
| **M** | Median | Target floor: bring liabilities up to ~50th percentile |
| **S** | Superpowers | Your strongest sense — identify it, then make it world-class |

---

## ⚡ The Three PM Senses

The foundation of the LMS framework — every PM operates through three core senses:

### 1. Product Sense
- Ability to make correct decisions about your product at **macro** and **micro** scale
- Macro: Should we build this? Will this feature succeed?
- Micro: Is this button right? Is this email subject line right?
- Examples of great product sense: Steve Jobs, Stuart Butterfield (Slack/Flickr), Patrick Collison (Stripe)

### 2. Analytical Sense
- Breaking down complexity; working with numbers, metrics, data
- Making decisions from both quantitative and qualitative data
- Processing vast information with ease; structured thinking

### 3. Execution Sense
- Getting things done predictably
- Handling complex projects and seeing them to completion

### The Key Insight: You Cannot Be World-Class at All Three
> *"The very thing that makes you great at product sense will make you not so great at execution sense."*

- Your brain is wired for one — that's your natural superpower
- Analogy: A world-class tennis player is not world-class at guitar. That's not a flaw — it's how humans work.
- ~20% of PMs claim to be equally good at all three → this is almost never true

---

## 🏆 S — Superpowers: Identify & Amplify

**Step 1:** Honestly ask yourself — which of the three senses am I *best* at?

**Step 2:** Turn that strength into a true superpower — get even better at it

**The interview test:** "What is your superpower as a PM?" — 8 out of 10 talented PMs fumble this answer. Know yours.

---

## ⚠️ L — Liabilities: Fix to the Median (Not to World-Class)

### Why PMs Can't Just "Focus on Strengths"
- Standard career advice: double down on strengths, ignore weaknesses
- This works for engineers (a great frontend engineer doesn't need to become a great backend engineer)
- **It does NOT work for PMs** — you need to be functional across all three senses

### The Target: 50th Percentile
- You don't need to be world-class at your liabilities
- You just need to bring them from the bottom 20% up to ~50th percentile
- This makes you a **well-rounded PM** who can work across product types and stages

### Why This Matters
- If you don't fix liabilities: you can only succeed on products that play to your superpower
- As scope grows or product stage changes, you'll hit a ceiling
- Later-stage products demand execution + analytical sense; 0→1 products demand product sense

## 🛠️ Real Example: Fixing Execution Sense at Google (2008)

> *"I realized I was not very good at running meetings... people at Google were extremely good at it."*

**The approach:**
1. Identified the specific gap: running team meetings
2. Asked manager to recommend PMs who were great at it
3. Sat in the corner of their meetings and observed — took notes on what "great" looked like
4. Modified techniques to fit his own style and personality
5. Result: went from bottom 20% → top 40–50% in **a matter of days**

**The lesson:** Fixing a liability doesn't require massive investment — targeted observation + deliberate practice works fast.

---

## 📋 How to Apply the LMS Framework

1. **Assess honestly** — where do you rank across product sense, analytical sense, execution sense?
2. **Name your superpower** — which sense is your strongest? Invest in making it exceptional.
3. **Identify your liabilities** — which senses are below median? These need targeted work.
4. **Build a specific plan** — read a book, work on a product type that exercises the weak sense, observe people who are great at it
5. **Repeat as a leader** — apply this framework to your team, not just yourself

*The through-line: Most PM feedback is generic and process-driven. The LMS Framework gives you a precise, honest self-assessment tool: know your superpower (S), identify what's holding you back (L), and bring weaknesses to functional median (M). You don't need to be world-class at everything — you need to be exceptional at one thing and competent at the rest.*
