*Lenny's Podcast*

## 🎙️ Guest & Context
- **Jeffrey Pfeffer** — Professor of Organizational Behavior at Stanford Graduate School of Business
- Teaches **Paths to Power** — one of the two most popular/oversubscribed courses in the entire MBA program (the other being Touchy-Feely)
- Author of four books on power, most recent: **Seven Rules of Power**
- 50+ years in education
- As one student described the class: *"The cod liver oil of the Graduate School of Business — you know it's good for you, but you feel a little nervous about it"*

## 🤔 Why This Makes People Uncomfortable (And Why It Matters Anyway)

### The Discomfort
> *"The realities of what it takes to get power bear almost no resemblance to what you're taught in Sunday school, how your parents raised you, or how we think the world ought to be."*

- We see people with power using it for bad → confuse the tool with how it's been used
- A hammer can hit someone on the head OR build a house
- A knife can be used by a mugger OR by a breast cancer surgeon to cure cancer
- *"We've confused the tool for how it has been used"*

### Why It Still Matters
- Gerald Ferris developed a **Political Skill Scale** — empirical research shows political skill is associated with:
  - Higher salary
  - Getting promoted
  - Being happy in your career
  - Being happy in your job
  - Being less stressed
- *"The reason why you should pay attention to this is because it leads to a lot of good things"*

### Who Needs It Most
- People from backgrounds that put them at a disadvantage
- Example: Jeffrey goes to Nashville to speak to people of color in the NFL trying to rise up the ranks
- *"These are folks who will not succeed unless they learn power skills, because the world is stacked against them in lots of ways"*

### The Opening Quote of the Book
> *"If you want power to be used for good, more good people need to have power."*

## 📋 The Seven Rules of Power

1. **Get out of your own way**
2. **Break the rules**
3. **Show up in a powerful fashion** (appear powerful)
4. **Build a powerful brand**
5. **Network relentlessly**
6. **Use your power**
7. **Success excuses almost everything** (what you did to get there will be forgiven, forgotten, or both)

---

## 💡 Rule 1: Get Out of Your Own Way

### The Two Main Ways People Block Themselves

**1. Moral discomfort with power**
- If you think power is dirty/evil, you won't do what you need to do to be successful
- The discomfort itself is the problem — it prevents action
- *"That is one way in which we get in our own way"*

**2. Imposter Syndrome**
- Believing you were the admissions mistake, the hiring mistake
- Surrounded by people you think are smarter and better
- Manifests as **pre-emptory apology**: *"I don't know if this comment is going to be useful..."*
  - If it's not going to be useful, don't say it
  - If it is useful, don't apologize for it
- *"Pardon me for interrupting. Pardon me for taking up your time. Stop all this apologizing."*
- If you got the job, you're probably not just qualified — you're probably overqualified

### The Leakage Problem
> *"If Lenny doesn't think he deserves the job, then maybe I shouldn't think he deserves the job either."*

- Attitude leaks out — others pick up on your self-assessment
- Don't use descriptors of yourself that disempower you

### The "Being Liked" Trap
- Famous quote from Gary Loveman (ran Harrah's/Caesars): **"If you want to be liked, get a dog"**
- You don't want to be intentionally disliked (don't violate Bob Sutton's No Asshole Rule)
- But you are hired to get a job done — not to be liked
- The neurosurgeon example: *"He's doing microsurgery on my spine. I really don't care about his personality or his office furniture."*
- **Prioritize competence and respect over being liked**
- Don't actively try to be disliked; just don't prioritize being liked

### The Class Journey: Four Stages
1. **Denial** — "It doesn't work in my culture/organization/religion"
2. **Anger** — "How can you teach us this stuff?"
3. **Sadness** — "I actually believe this and I'm depressed"
4. **Acceptance** — The goal

## 🏷️ Rule 4: Build a Powerful Brand

### Why It's Necessary
> *"No one is going to promote Lenny if they don't know who the hell you are."*

- Fewer positions at the top than at the bottom — the world is hierarchical
- You cannot choose what is not in your head
- Visibility without substance = people know you're useless
- Substance without visibility = no one knows the substance you have
- **You need both**

### Case Study: Laura Chau (VC Partner at Canaan)
- Made partner at a VC firm after just 4 years (very fast)
- Doesn't work for Andreessen, Sequoia, or Greylock — no built-in brand recognition
- What she did:
  - Writing
  - Started a podcast (invited influential people on)
  - Contributed to a book
  - Helped people out
  - Hosted networking dinners
  - Played up her distinctive physical presence (tall Asian woman — 6'1" in heels)
  - Developed a unique, strategic personal style
- *"She thinks very strategically about how am I going to dress, how am I going to look, how am I going to show up"*

### Case Study: Keith Ferrazzi at Deloitte
- Not good at spreadsheets, didn't like them
- Instead: started the Lincoln Quality Award, worked to take Deloitte's brand recognition from 1-2% to 30%
- Result: offered partner and first CMO position at Deloitte

### Case Study: Tristan Walker at Foursquare
- Sent emails to founder → ignored
- Bold move: started signing up partnerships himself
- One day founder sees he signed up Starbucks → *"Maybe I ought to hire this guy"*

### The Reframe
- Not self-promotion — it's **amplifying the impact of the team you work with**
- It's **scaling yourself** by sharing what you know

### The "Doing Power" Homework
- Students must do something during the quarter to put principles into practice
- Example: Derek Kan's 2012 project was to get appointed as head of economic policy for the Romney campaign
  - Was offered the job, turned it down
  - Became #3 in the Transportation Department
  - By end of Trump administration: essentially ran the $6 trillion US budget as deputy director of OMB
  - All within 6 years of business school

## 🚫 Rule 2: Break the Rules

### Why Breaking Rules Creates Power

**1. You stand out and become memorable**
- Doing something unexpected makes you memorable
- Being memorable is essential for advancement

**2. Rules were made by people favored by the existing rules**
- Business strategy parallel: disruption
- Southwest Airlines: no hub and spoke
- Amazon: incredible customer service
- Whole Foods: optimize for local taste, not cost
- *"If you are going to be successful, you have to do what plays to your strengths and causes you to stand out"*

### The "Just Ask" Rule
- Conventional wisdom: don't ask, show self-sufficiency
- Research by Frances Flynn and Vanessa Lake: *"If You Need Help, Just Ask"*
- People overestimate how many people they'll have to ask to get help
- *"What is the worst that could happen? If you ask and they say no, you are no worse off than had you not asked."*
- Jeffrey's personal example: asked his wife out repeatedly — *"I asked, I asked, I asked"*

### Case Study: Jason Calacanis
- Defies conventional VC wisdom almost completely
- Makes many little bets, not a few big bets
- Runs a very lean operation — no partners (can't be fired by partners)
- Started in journalism → asked smart people questions → became incredibly smart and insightful about the internet industry
- *"Nobody likes him, but that's okay"* — that's power rule #1 in action

## 👔 Rule 3: Show Up in a Powerful Fashion

### The Science of Appearance
- Evidence suggests we respond mostly to **how people look**, secondarily to **how they sound**, and least to **the content of what they say**
- Tall people earn more money (controlling for other factors)
- Attractive people (not too attractive) earn more money
- We respond affectively — descended from primates, controlled by emotions

### The BP vs. Goldman Demonstration
- Shows 60 seconds (34 seconds for Lloyd Blankfein) of Tony Hayward (BP) and Lloyd Blankfein (Goldman) with no sound
- Even without knowing who they are: you can tell who keeps their job and who loses it
- Just from physical appearance and body language

### Steve Jobs Was Made
- Regis McKenna and his team created Steve Jobs the presenter
- *"When Steve Jobs began, Steve Jobs couldn't convince you to buy water if you were dying of thirst. He could not give a presentation to save his life."*
- *"These are skills. This is not about personality."*

### Jack Valenti: The Five-Foot-Two Giant
- Head of MPAA for 38 years, consistently rated #1 or #2 most effective lobbyist in Washington DC
- Never appeared before Congress with notes
- *"I wanted them to believe, which was true, that I was in complete control and complete mastery of the material"*
- A woman in class said after seeing him: *"He feels taller than he is"*
- Because of his presence, body language, Texas drawl, how he moved on stage — he felt bigger

### Specific Behaviors That Signal Power

| Behavior | Why It Works |
|---|---|
| Don't read from notes | Shows mastery, enables eye contact, signals sincerity |
| Make eye contact | Signals confidence and engagement |
| Keep it brief | Commands attention |
| Anger (used strategically) | Outside the norm, signals you're not afraid |
| Don't apologize | Signals confidence |
| More gestures | Signals engagement and energy |
| Open body posture | Signals confidence and openness |
| Louder voice | Commands attention |
| Invade personal space (gently) | Touch on arm/shoulder, handshakes |
| More speaking time | Signals authority |
| Longer gazing time | Signals confidence |
| Disinhibited laughter/humor | *"If you can get people to laugh, you can tell them anything"* — Salman Rushdie |

## 🌐 Rule 5: Network Relentlessly

### The Right Frame for Networking
> *"The first principle of networking is generosity. What can I do for you? How can I be helpful? Who can I introduce you to?"*

- Most people find networking "dirty" because they think about it wrong
- It's not transactional — it's about connecting people who benefit from being connected

### The Power of Being a Broker
- What does a VC do? Connects people with ideas to people with money
- What does an investment banker do? Connects buyers and sellers of capital
- What does a real estate agent do? Connects buyers and sellers of houses
- *"If leadership is getting things done through other people, it seems like common sense that the more other people you know, the more you'll be able to get done"*

### Case Study: Omid Kordestani (Employee #11 at Google)
- Persian immigrant, engineer background, graduated Stanford
- At Netscape in mid-90s, not making progress
- Radical decision: **stopped doing his job** and spent all his time networking
  - First within Netscape: *"If you're well-connected to senior leaders, they don't really care if you do your job or not"*
  - Found others to do his actual work
  - Then drove through Silicon Valley talking to everyone
- By 1998: when Google (10 engineers) needed their first business person, they asked everyone they knew
- *"There is of course one name that appears on every list"* — Omid Kordestani
- Became employee #11 at Google → made $2.5 billion

### The Weak Ties Insight
- **Strong ties** (family, close friends, colleagues): know the same things and people you do
- **Weak ties** (more distant connections): provide non-redundant information and access to people you don't know
- Mark Granovetter's research: *"The Strength of Weak Ties"*
- Jobs gotten through network referrals are much better jobs
- The best jobs often came from people who were NOT particularly close to the job seeker
- *"They had a view into the ecosystem that others didn't have"*

### The 10-Person Exercise
1. Write a list of 10 people who, if you knew them and they knew you, would be important for what you're trying to accomplish
2. For each person, figure out how you're going to meet them
3. Not necessarily at a networking event — dinners, lunch, articles, introductions

### Jeffrey's Networking Example
- Connected Esther Wojcicki (interested in teen depression) with Leanne Williams (Precision Psychiatry researcher)
- Leanne needs funding; Esther has money
- Esther needs cutting-edge research; Leanne has it
- *"This is not some icky thing. This is connecting people who benefit from being connected."*

## ⚡ Rule 6: Use Your Power

### The Self-Fulfilling Prophecy of Power
> *"If I believe you're powerful, you will become more powerful. If I believe you have no power, you will wind up with almost no power."*

- People want to be associated with success
- The more you get done → more people want to work with you → more promotions → more resources
- Nobody gives you a job if the last five similar jobs you couldn't get done
- Mobilize resources → get things done → get more resources

### The Ambivalence Problem
- Herminia Ibarra's HBR article: woman promoted in a drug company tells her new reports she's not sure why she got the job or if she deserves it
- Result: she didn't do well — got in her own way
- *"You are put in a position of power in order to make things happen"*

### Power Attracts Power
- Chimpanzee Politics parallel: we are the same as chimps in how we're attracted to power
- People are attracted to power — this accounts for a lot of political behavior

## 🏆 Rule 7: Success Excuses Almost Everything

### Why This Rule Exists
- Many people fear: *"If I do this, at the end I will be brought low"* — the Icarus myth
- Life is NOT homeostatic — it doesn't self-correct
- Life is actually **self-fulfilling**

### Examples of Forgetting and Forgiving
- **Bill Gates**: people forget he stole the code on which Microsoft was built
- **Jeffrey Epstein**: after being convicted of sex offenses, still having dinners with royalty and NY media elite
- **Martha Stewart**: served time in jail; her brand has never been more valuable
- **Lindsey Graham**: said nasty things about Trump, became his biggest acolyte
  - His answer: *"I want to be relevant. He's the President. If I want to get legislation passed, I need him on my side."*
- The man who took a company into bankruptcy, was fined, walked away with $700 million
  - *"$700 million makes you a big deal"*
  - *"People want to be close to money, power, and success, and they will overlook your flaws to be close to you"*

### The Trump Connection
- Jeffrey originally considered writing a book about why Trump is so successful
- Realized: *"No one's going to pay attention if it's 'here's how to be like Trump'"*
- Trump exemplifies all seven rules — not because he read the book, but through trial and error

## 🔬 The Knowing-Doing Gap

### The Core Problem
- If you learn French and never speak French, you'll forget it
- If you learn power principles and never practice them, you'll forget them
- *"If all you do is read, you're not going to make very much progress"*

### How Jeffrey Bridges the Gap in Class
- Every topic → a practice assignment
- Executive coaches work with students (23 students per coach)
- Assignments include:
  1. **Goal setting**: Why are you here? What would success look like?
  2. **Self-assessment**: Rate yourself on seven attributes of power; create a development plan
  3. **Resource creation**: What can you create in 10 weeks that gives you leverage?
  4. **Networking**: The 10-person exercise
  5. **Acting with power**: Film a 60-90 second video representing BP better than Tony Hayward; get peer feedback
  6. **Personal brand statement**: Write it, get feedback, rewrite it

### The Coaching Principle
- *"If I want you to swim the English Channel, we're going to start by swimming in pools and make every day a little bit more challenging"*
- Don't start by saying you'll network like Keith Ferrazzi or be as bold as Jason Calacanis
- Begin where you can begin, push yourself out of your comfort zone
- Parallel to Touchy-Feely's 15% Rule: push yourself 15% beyond your comfort zone

## 💊 The Laura Esserman Story: Power for Good

### The Setup
- Laura Esserman: breast cancer surgeon, getting MBA at Stanford while having her first child and practicing medicine full time
- 1993: took Jeffrey's class
- Challenge to Jeffrey: *"You have cases on all these people doing evil things. Why don't you ever write a case about somebody doing good things?"*

### The Class Evisceration
- Students were hard on her — she wouldn't network, wouldn't schmooze
- *"I don't have time for schmoozing"*
- She was pissed after class

### The Physics Conversation
> *"You are creating friction. With enough force you can overcome the friction. If you reduce the friction, just think how much more you could accomplish."*

- She looked at him and changed

### The Results
- Time Magazine 2016: 100 Most Influential People in the World
- Won every major cancer award
- The Athena Project: collecting data on diagnosis, treatment, and outcomes for 150,000 patients in the UC Healthcare System
- Dick Blum (Dianne Feinstein's husband): *"I've learned that at the end of the day when Laura asks you to do something, you may say no, but at the end of the day, you're going to do it anyway. Save yourself the aggravation."*
- Jeffrey: *"Of all the things I've done in my life, the thing I'm most proud of is creating Laura Esserman"*

### The Lesson
> *"You want to change medicine? Medicine is not going to change without the application of power and influence. If change was going to happen, it would've happened already."*

## ⚖️ The Price of Power: The Autonomy Trade-Off

### James March's Insight
> *"You can have power or you can have autonomy, but you cannot have both."*

### What Power Costs
- **Time**: Every hour booked, schedule controlled by assistants
- **Privacy**: Put under a microscope — car, relationships, associations, spending
- **Scrutiny**: Things that were overlooked before become public
- **Trust dilemma**: Are people praising you because of you or because of your position?
- **Family cost**: Rudy Crew's daughter in the room — not a dry eye when she described what it's like to be the child of someone in power

### Jeffrey's Choice
- Chose autonomy over power
- Controls his own schedule — Outlook calendar not on any website
- *"Time goes in only one direction. I want control of my time."*
- Did one academic administrative job → led biggest transformation in Stanford's history → didn't like it → never again

3. **Start small** — push yourself a little, then a little more
4. **Do it** — reading is not enough; you cannot learn tennis by reading about tennis
