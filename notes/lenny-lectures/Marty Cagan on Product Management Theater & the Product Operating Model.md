## 🌡️ Why Marty Is Getting Spicier

### The Convergence of Simultaneous Disruptions
Marty identifies multiple macro forces hitting at the same time — a recipe for chaos:

1. **Pandemic overhiring + lowered hiring bar** — companies hired too many people at reduced quality standards
2. **Rising cost of capital** — financial environment changed dramatically, scrutiny on headcount increased
3. **Generative AI impact** — NVIDIA CEO saying "don't learn programming" creates uncertainty at minimum; real disruption likely, timeline uncertain
4. **Team size bloat** — especially outside Silicon Valley, roles have gotten out of hand
5. **Remote work** — velocity and innovation have taken a real hit; companies struggling with both
6. **Process-heavy cultures** — SAFe, scrum misapplication, agile theater widespread outside Silicon Valley

> *"The bigger surprise honestly is it's taken so long for so many companies to realize what is going on."*

## 🎭 Product Management Theater: The Full Breakdown

### What It Is
- Companies carrying product manager titles without doing the actual product manager job
- People doing project management work while calling it product management
- *"They carry this title product manager because the whole world knows it's cool, but they're not doing any of the role and they don't have any of the skills"*

### The Ridiculous Roles Proliferation
Marty's list of roles that have gotten out of hand:
- Agile coaches
- Product owners
- Product ops (in many implementations)
- Business analysts
- Scrum masters
- Every flavor of project manager
- Assistant product manager types

> *"I go into some companies and honestly I can't believe all the ridiculous roles that they have."*

Referenced his article from ~10 years ago called **"Epic Waste"** — was pointing this out then, still happening now.

### Why PMs Get a Bad Reputation
- The anti-PM sentiment ("I don't want PMs at my startup") comes from people's experience with **feature team PMs**
- Feature team PMs are doing project management, not product management
- Engineers and designers often say: *"We'd rather do it ourselves than deal with this person that's got this complex and trying to be the boss of everybody and they really don't contribute anything"*
- *"They're dramatically overpaid for the value they provide"* — brutal but accurate for feature team PMs
- This is the biggest clue a team is a feature team: when people don't find value in the PM

## 🏭 Feature Teams vs. Empowered Product Teams

### Feature Team Characteristics
- Given a **roadmap of output** — features, projects
- Features come from executives, big customers, wherever
- Asked to design, build, test, deploy the feature
- Usually given dates and timeframes
- Measure of success: **did you ship it?**
- *"It is a lot easier to deliver output than it is to deliver outcomes"*

### Empowered Product Team Characteristics
- Given **problems to solve** — customer problems or business problems
- Usually 1–2 hard problems per quarter (plus keep-the-lights-on work)
- Measure of success: **did it solve the problem?**
- *"You just don't get points for shipping, you get points for delivering the value"*

### The CEO/CFO Frame
> *"It's about time to money more than time to market. We know how to do time to market. The harder part is time to money — and that's what a product team really does."*

### Why You Only Need a PM on an Empowered Team
- Feature team = project management role → engineers or designers can cover it
- Empowered team = you've signed up for an outcome → you need someone responsible for **value and viability**
- That requires skills engineers and designers almost never have (not a knock — just different skillsets)
- A real PM on an empowered team is too busy doing their actual job to micromanage designers or irritate developers

## 📊 The PM Spectrum

| Role | Description | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Product Owner** | Backlog administrator in a delivery process | Shouldn't be a dedicated person; senior engineer could do it better |
| **Feature Team PM** | Does more than backlog admin; lots of project management | Some value but dramatically overpaid; project management ≠ product management |
| **Empowered PM** | Responsible for value and viability; creator not facilitator | The real job; what companies actually need |

## 🛠️ What a Real PM Actually Does

### The Core Responsibility: Value + Viability
- **Value** = for the customer (does it solve their problem?)
- **Viability** = for the business (can you sell it, market it, is it legal, can you service it, compliance?)
- Engineers own **feasibility** (can it be built?)
- Designers own **usability** (can customers use it?)

### Specific Skills Required

**1. Deep customer expertise**
- Marty wasn't allowed to take a PM role until he had visited **30 customers in person** (15 US, 15 Europe)
- *"Those 30 customers changed my life because I thought I knew our customers and I really didn't"*

**2. Data expertise**
- How is the product being used?
- How is usage changing over time?
- How is it being purchased?

**3. Business/viability knowledge**
- Compliance issues
- Sales constraints
- Marketing requirements
- Financial cost issues
- Monetization
- Legal constraints
- Go-to-market in general

**4. Market understanding**
- Deep knowledge of the competitive landscape and market dynamics

### The Creator vs. Facilitator Distinction
> *"On a real empowered product team, product manager is a creator, not a facilitator."*

- Marty cringes when PMs say "my job is to ask why"
- *"What do you do for the rest of the week besides the 10 minutes it takes you to say why?"*
- The "why" comes from product strategy anyway — PMs don't even own that
- Side-by-side creation with design and engineering to come up with solutions

### What Product Owners Learn vs. What They Should Know
- CSPO/PSPO certification teaches: how to manage a backlog in Jira
- That's analogous to learning how to operate Google Docs
- *"Of course that's not the job. That's something we do every day, but it's not the job — just like developers are in Jira every day but that's not their job"*

## 🔄 The Propagation Problem: Why Bad Advice Dominates

### The Self-Propagating Cycle
1. Someone posts a question in a product community
2. Well-meaning people jump in with what they learned at their company
3. Most companies are feature teams
4. Therefore most advice is feature team advice
5. Person asking gets wrong advice, thanks everyone, propagates further

> *"It becomes self-propagating. And what are you going to do? Is somebody going to police these boards? I don't want to do that."*

### The Content Landscape Problem
- ~90% of online product management content is from the feature team world or worse
- Certifications (CSPO, PSPO) teach project management, not product management
- Most books earnestly describe what people learned, not what good companies do
- Conference speakers proportionally represent the majority (feature team world)
- Exception Marty highlights: **Teresa Torres's Continuous Discovery Habits** — *"Good book. Try to get everybody to read that."*

### Advice for Navigating This
- **Buyer beware** — use critical thinking
- Research your future manager specifically: where did they work? What products did they build?
- *"The most important thing is who's going to coach you — not so much the company"*
- Take ownership of your career — don't just consume whatever content is most available

## 🤖 AI's Impact on Product Management

### Short-Term (Now)
- Improving communication and writing
- Strategy feedback: "here's my strategy, give me feedback"
- PRD/spec writing
- Bug triage
- *"It's hard to think of something you can't use it for. The harder question is what is it good for?"*

### Marty's Reversed Advice on Using AI
- **Old advice**: Start with ChatGPT, go from there
- **New advice**: Think through the answer first, put something down, THEN use ChatGPT to challenge/improve it
- Why reversed: *"People are taking what they get too literally, too seriously, too much value, and they were heading off in a wrong direction and then optimizing that"*

### Long-Term Vulnerability by Role Type

| Role | AI Vulnerability |
|---|---|
| **Backlog administrator/Product Owner** | Very high — *"Already people are doing that. It's only a matter of time."* |
| **Feature team PM** | High — *"Very little going on that is truly value add. Most are administrative things."* |
| **Empowered PM (value + viability)** | Lower — viability questions become MORE important with AI |

### Why Viability Becomes More Important with AI
- Probabilistic software vs. deterministic software — what's acceptable?
- Legal and ethical implications of AI outputs
- Mission-critical decisions: is a probabilistic answer okay?
- *"A lot more is landing squarely on the product manager than in general in the past"*

### The Upskilling Imperative
> *"If you are fundamentally a backlog administrator, good luck protecting that. That is not a good job prospect."*

## 🏢 The Meta/Zuck Discussion: Top-Down vs. Empowered

### The Common Misunderstanding
- Meta is often perceived as bottom-up (teams run experiments, lots of autonomy)
- CTO described it as actually very top-down (Zuck and execs set strategy and big bets)
- Marty's take: **both descriptions are correct and not contradictory**

### The Right Frame: Leaders Doing Their Job + Teams Doing Their Job
- Product leaders do product strategy — the focus decisions, the bets
- Product teams get those bets and have latitude to figure out HOW to solve them
- *"Empowerment does not mean you set up this product team and they go decide what to work on. That would just be anarchy."*
- Handing a team a roadmap of features = very top-down (bad)
- Leaders setting strategy + teams solving problems = empowerment (good)

### On Zuck Specifically
> *"I'm not the biggest fan of Meta, but Zuck is very good at product, very good. That's the problem in the world. He's so good at it."*

## 📈 The PM Role Shift Post-ZIRP (Nikhyl Singhal's Observation)

### Nikhyl's View
- Past decade: PMs mostly responsible for growth — optimizing existing products with PMF
- Post-ZIRP: return to building, finding PMF, discovery

### Marty's Nuanced Response
- Partially agrees — but the shift is really from feature teams to product teams
- What Nikhyl calls "growth hacking" Marty calls "optimization"
- AB testing call-to-action changes = optimization, not discovery
- Teams do optimization-only because: (1) given a roadmap with no room for anything else, or (2) scared to break things
- *"I don't think it's tied to interest rates. I think it's more tied to the quality of the leadership and the need of the business to do more than optimization."*

## 📚 TRANSFORMED: The New Book

### Why This Book Exists
- Most common question after INSPIRED: *"I love this, I want to do this — but have you ever seen our company? We are so far away from that."*
- INSPIRED = what good product looks like
- EMPOWERED = product leadership, vision, strategy, coaching
- TRANSFORMED = **how to actually change** to work this way

### What's Different About This Book
- Written for **non-product people too** — CEOs, CFOs, heads of sales, anyone who cares about the company
- All case studies are from **outside Silicon Valley** — companies that had to change dramatically
- Pre-internet companies that transformed: Trainline (UK), Almosafer (Saudi Arabia — owns 80%+ of travel market over Expedia), companies in Brazil, Virginia, healthcare, car sales, fitness
- *"Some of these innovations are as impressive as anything I've seen Amazon do — and that's saying a lot"*

### Three Goals of the Book
1. Help people understand what it really means to move to this way of working (no fluff)
2. Help people believe transformation is possible (it's not easy, but it's possible)
3. Get people excited about what they'll be able to do after transforming

### The Name: Product Operating Model
- Marty dodged naming it for 20 years — just said "work like the best vs. the rest"
- Avoided "product-led" and "product-driven" — gives wrong message, rest of company thinks it's a power grab
- **Product operating model** = non-threatening, conceptual model (not a process), set of principles
- Shortened to **"product model"**

## 🔑 The Product Operating Model: 20 Principles

### Three Big Dimensions

**1. How you decide what to work on (Product Strategy)**
- What problems to solve
- What the leaders do — their job
- Annual planning is just budgeting; real strategy is ongoing

**2. How you solve problems (Product Discovery)**
- Do you have skills to do real product discovery?
- Coming up with solutions that work for customer AND business

**3. How you build, test, and deploy (Product Delivery)**
- Reliable, demonstrable
- Generates outcomes you need

### Four Core Competencies (Most Companies Don't Have These Roles Institutionalized)

| Competency | What It Actually Means |
|---|---|
| **Real Product Manager** | Responsible for value and viability; not product owner, not feature team PM |
| **Real Product Designer** | Service design, interaction design, visual design, user research — full spectrum |
| **Real Tech Lead** | Not just a senior engineer — a true technical leader |
| **Real Product Leader** | Manager of product/design/engineering who can coach people AND do real product strategy |

> *"Most companies have people with those names, but they don't have those roles institutionalized."*

### Sample Principles by Category

**Cultural Principles:**
- Innovation is more important than predictability
- Learning is more important than failure avoidance
- Principles are more important than process

**Team Principles:**
- Empowered with problems to solve (not features to build)
- Real ownership — it's theirs

**Discovery Principles:**
- Address product risks proactively
- Embrace quick experimentation
- Test ideas responsibly

**Delivery Principles:**
- Small, frequent, uncoupled releases (CI/CD)
- Instrumentation of everything
- Monitoring of everything

### On Release Cadence
- Good companies release ~20 times per day at the team level
- Marketing releases (like Airbnb's seasonal launches) are separate — batching for messaging makes sense
- *"By the time they message it, it's live. It's been coming out. We may have released some things dark, but we've got it in production solid."*
- Quarterly releases = cannot take care of customers, cannot learn at needed pace, quality will be terrible

## 🔧 Product Ops: The Nuanced View

### What Product Ops Is (and Isn't)
- Analogous to DevOps and DesignOps — not the same as "product operating model"
- Good version: **user research + data analysts** brought together under one leader
- This isn't new — companies have had these teams for 20+ years

### Red Flags in Product Ops
- Focused on "process and governance" → *"That's a huge red flag. Run, don't walk away."*
- Used as assistants to product managers who claim too much work
  - *"It's not even enough for your job [as a feature team PM]"*
  - Very vulnerable role right now
- Product leaders not doing their job → hire product ops to do it for them → wrong

### The Associate PM / Product Owner Anti-Pattern
- Companies keep finding creative ways to give PMs assistants
- First: associate product managers
- Then: product owners alongside product managers (huge anti-pattern)
- Now: product ops people to do the "dirty work"
- *"I would not want to be one of those people because I think they're very vulnerable right now"*

## 🚀 Advice for Founders on Hiring PMs

### Don't Hire Too Soon
- Marty actively discourages early PM hires
- If using PM as project manager: overpaying, but okay for some project management help
- If real PM: that's the **founder's job** — value and viability IS what founders do
- Bringing in a real PM too soon = too many cooks in the kitchen, causes conflict

### When to Hire
- After product-market fit is a better time
- Heuristic: **~20–25 engineers** is when a co-founder can no longer effectively be the product person for everything
- *"While it's small, it usually is most useful just to look at the number of engineers"*

### The Pattern Marty Sees
- Founders say "I don't need PMs, they'll slow us down"
- Hire one → *"Oh wow, this is amazing. Why didn't I do this earlier?"*
- This happens when they hire a **real** PM, not a feature team PM

## 🌍 The Feature Team Trap: Agency and Escape

### The Victim Mentality Problem
> *"Too many people in our industry view themselves as a victim of their company. They're stuck in a feature team and there's nothing they can do about it other than quit."*

### What Individual PMs Can Do
1. **Self-assess honestly** — are you a product owner, feature team PM, or empowered PM?
2. **Raise your skills** — it's not that hard if you're motivated
3. **Demonstrate value** — at minimum, company will appreciate and promote you
4. **Advocate for change** — can happen from the ground up; *"Hey, why don't we try running a set of teams this way?"*

### What Product Leaders Can Do
- Even more agency than ICs to change a company
- The biggest shame: most product leaders aren't doing this
- They have enormous ability to transform how their company works

## 💡 The B2B Sales-Driven Company Question

### Is It Ever Okay to Be a Feature Factory?
Marty's answer: No, and here's why:

- *"This is not an accident why most B2B software is such crap. It is horrible."*
- Oracle, SAP: massively valuable but does anyone actually like those products?
- Sales-driven product exists mostly because CEOs aren't product people
- Usually doesn't change until a good product company takes away their customers
- *"An empowered product team can do everything a feature team can do and more"*
- TRANSFORMED includes a case study of a classic sales-driven financial services company that transformed → dramatically improved things for the sales organization too
