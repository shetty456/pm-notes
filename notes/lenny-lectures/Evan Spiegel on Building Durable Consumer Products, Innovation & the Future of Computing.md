## 🏗️ Why Consumer Social Products Are So Hard to Build

### The Core Problem: Distribution
> *"So much of consumer technology focuses on 'do I have product market fit?' People don't spend nearly enough time thinking about distribution and figuring out distribution."*

**Why it was easier in 2011:**
- Mobile phones and app stores were just getting started
- People were downloading lots of new apps and excited to try new services
- Instagram launched just a year before Snapchat — real appetite for new apps

**Why it's harder now:**
- People aren't downloading as many apps as they used to
- The two recent successes (TikTok and Threads) both solved distribution, not product

**How TikTok solved distribution:**
- Spent billions subsidizing both sides of their video marketplace
- Paid creators to make videos AND acquired customers to watch them
- *"I actually thought that was really innovative"*

**How Threads solved distribution:**
- Leveraged Meta's existing distribution across all their other products

### Snapchat's Early Distribution Insight
- Conventional wisdom: network effects mean bigger networks are stickier and unbeatable
- Snapchat's insight: **connecting you to the right people matters more than connecting you to the most people**
- *"If you could just connect someone not to all their friends, but to their best friend, to their partner, to their spouse, the people that they cared most about in the world — that's where the majority of the value is in the network"*
- This allowed them to grow despite much larger competitors

### The Future of Distribution
- New platforms create new distribution opportunities (mobile created Uber, Snapchat, etc.)
- Glasses/AR could be the next platform shift with new distribution opportunities
- AI won't solve distribution — it remains the key moat

## 🔄 Why Software Is Not a Moat (And What Is)

### The Core Lesson (Learned 15 Years Ago)
> *"15 years ago, we essentially learned that software is not a moat — which is something that everyone is discovering today with AI."*

- All software features can be easily cloned by competitors
- Patents don't really protect you in software

### What Actually Creates Defensibility

**1. Ecosystems and Platforms**
- Relationships between creators and Snapchatters
- AR platform where developers have built millions of lenses
- *"It's very easy to copy software features, it's very hard to copy or replicate a full ecosystem or a platform"*

**2. Hardware**
- Fully vertically integrated stack around augmented reality
- *"Really really challenging to copy"*

**3. Network Effects (Necessary but Not Sufficient)**
- Important piece of the puzzle
- But not enough on their own when software can be cloned
- Need to combine with platform/ecosystem thinking

## 🥽 The Hardware Bet: Why Specs

### The Philosophical Foundation
> *"I love computers. But in many ways they isolate us from one another. They actually take us out of our social interactions."*

- Phones require you to look down, even when with friends
- People spend 7-8 hours a day on screens — something has to change
- The opportunity: **technology that brings us together rather than removing us from the real world**

### The Roadmap from Spectacles to Specs
1. **Early Spectacles (~2014)**: Get the camera off the phone, allow easier snapping in the world
2. **Added second camera**: Get depth perception
3. **Added display**: Overlay digital objects into the world
4. **2024 generation**: Released operating system allowing developers to build full-featured software
5. **Specs (consumer launch)**: Full consumer product

### Why AR Glasses vs. Heads-Up Display Glasses
- Specs anchor content in the world (not a screen stuck to your face)
- Heads-up display glasses: *"When you're looking at the little heads-up display in the corner, you're actually looking at like your friend's crotch while you're talking to them"*
- Receiving phone notifications on your face is not a compelling value proposition
- The goal: show people new ways to hang out and connect with friends

### The Multiplayer Vision
- Specs are designed for connection, not isolation
- Multiplayer features built in from the start
- Contrast with VR: you're in a shared environment with others, not isolated

## 💡 How Snap Innovates: The Organizational Model

### The Loonshots Framework (Safi Bahcall)
> *"If you want to deliver a product at scale, you usually need quite a large organization. A large organization needs hierarchy and structure. As soon as you have that, people become very focused on getting a promotion — they become risk-averse."*

**Two types of organizations that need to coexist:**
1. **Large, structured, hierarchical** — needed to serve customers at scale, operational rigor
2. **Small, flat, non-hierarchical** — needed for innovation, risk-taking, new ideas

**The leadership challenge:**
- The two groups naturally create tension and criticize each other
- Leaders must create a healthy, functioning relationship between them
- *"The companies that are very successful actually have both types of organizations inside their company"*

### Snap's Implementation

**The Design Team (9-12 people):**
- Completely flat structure — no one has a fancier title or role
- Flexibility to try new things and fail quickly
- No hierarchy, ideas can come from anywhere
- Evan meets with them for a couple hours every week to look at new work

**The Dialogue:**
- Engineers working on reliability and scale have great ideas
- Designers have great ideas
- *"As long as they're in dialogue, really great things can happen"*
- Modeled on the original Evan-Bobby relationship: design + engineering in constant dialogue

### The Velocity Principle
> *"If you want to have a good idea, you have to have lots of ideas."*

- Hundreds of ideas reviewed weekly
- Art school background: grinding expectation of making new work constantly + brutal critique process
- Stanford design background: empathy + iterative prototyping
- The combination: high-velocity making + deep customer empathy

### First Day Culture
> *"Your first day that you join the design team, you present work."*

- Sets the tone immediately: this team is about making things
- Removes preciousness around ideas
- When you're creating a thousand ideas, who cares that most aren't great?

### Designer Rotation
- Designers don't get stuck on specific products or verticals for long
- Rotate through different parts of the product to bring fresh perspectives
- Prevents boredom and brings new ideas to each area

## 🎨 How to Hire Great Designers

### The Portfolio-First Approach
> *"We make all of our hiring decisions just based off portfolio. I really don't care where you've worked or your level of experience."*

- Most people join right out of school, not from big tech companies

### What to Look For in a Portfolio

**1. Wide Range (The Art vs. Design Distinction)**
- Art: expressing your own personal point of view, distinct style
- Design: empathizing with your audience and building something that resonates with them
- *"Are they building stuff that all looks the same? Or are they building things that look really different, responsive to different sets of needs?"*
- Wide range = designer; consistent style = artist

**2. The Story Behind the Work**
- Ask them to pick anything from their portfolio they feel strongly about (love it or hate it)
- Ask them to tell the story of why they made it and what they learned
- Reveals their process for building and inventing
- Looking for people who have different ways of approaching problems

**3. Diverse Backgrounds**
- 3D animation, electrical engineering, etc.
- Different ways of seeing the world = strength

## 📖 The Stories Origin Story

### What Customers Were Asking For
- "Give me a send-all button so I can blast snaps to everybody"

### What Customers Were Actually Feeling
- Pressure from social media — everything is permanent, has likes and comments
- Only feel comfortable posting "pretty and perfect" things
- Loved Snapchat's freedom from judgment
- Found reverse chronological order strange (end of birthday party appeared first)

### What Snap Built (Not What Was Asked For)
- **Stories** — addressed all the underlying needs without building what was requested:
  - Easy sharing with all friends without spamming
  - No public metrics (no likes/comments)
  - Disappear after 24 hours — everyone starts fresh
  - Chronological order — the way humans have always told stories

> *"We listened to all of that, but we came up with something totally new and different that was responsive to the feedback without building exactly what they asked for. We empathized and then came up with something new."*

### The Iterative Process
- Started more around a status update concept
- Through talking to people and iterating, evolved into Stories
- Gestures (tap forward/backward), full screen, vertical content — all new at the time

## 📸 The Screenshot Detection Story

### The Problem
- Everyone said: "You can always take a screenshot. What do you mean photos disappear?"

### The Invention
- Discovered: pressing and holding to open a snap + taking a screenshot triggers a touch event
- The phone reports that your finger lost contact with the screen
- Apple didn't provide an API for screenshot detection, but this touch event was detectable
- Built notification system: sender gets notified when recipient takes a screenshot

### The Impact
- *"They didn't mind if somebody saved one of their snaps. They just wanted to know."*
- One of the first things that got real traction in early days
- Helped Snapchat spread as a new way of communicating

## 🤔 On Talking to Customers

### Evan's View (vs. Keith Rabois's "Don't Talk to Customers")
> *"You have to talk to customers. You should share your idea as quickly as possible, as frequently as possible. It doesn't mean you need to take people's advice or feedback, but it's really important to listen."*

- Not the survey model — that's not particularly helpful
- Going deep: talking with someone for an hour or two about how they use technology, how it fits in their lives
- *"Customers are an endless source of inspiration when it comes to building products"*

## 👨‍💼 On Product Managers

### The Early Philosophy (No PMs for First 200 Employees)
- Concern: traditional tech org structure makes designers produce visuals, not product direction
- Didn't want designers to be responsive to PMs saying "go make me some visuals"
- Wanted designers to do PM work themselves
- *"If you need PM support, why don't you just do it yourself? What's the big deal?"*
- This helped designers play a more active role in product development

### The Current Reality (At Scale)
- Legal requirements, trust and safety, data science — it takes a village
- PMs play an important coordination role
- Synthesizing data science analysis
- Bringing together working groups to solve problems
- *"Product managers play a really important role in bringing all those folks to the table"*

### Design as the Bottleneck (Intentional)
> *"Design actually has always operated as like a bottleneck at the company, which is incredibly important. It's intentional that things need to be approved by design to ship."*

- Sometimes annoys people and slows down shipping
- But results in a cohesive customer experience
- You can tell when an app has been built by teams responsible for different pages without a cohesive through line

## 🤖 AI at Snap

### The Jobs-to-Be-Done Framework for AI
- Started with jobs to be done for community and advertisers
- Snapchatters: download app, add close friends, use lenses
- Advertisers: enter ad platform, configure campaign, etc.
- *"By listing out all these jobs to be done, it became very clear where we could use agents"*

### Specific AI Implementations
- **Automated code review**: detected close to 10,000 bugs automatically
- **Shake to report**: on internal app, shake to report a problem → agents debug what happened, suggest a fix (soon: implement the fix)
- **Go-to-market agent**: product idea → spec → identify relevant stakeholders → risk analysis (legal, trust & safety) → blog post → visuals — all in one shot
- **Evan's personal agent** (built in Glean): combs through all dashboards, documents, data → surfaces what needs attention

### The Flat Leadership Vision
> *"What's been most exciting for me in terms of AI is that it's enabling the type of leadership structure that I've always wanted — very flat, very fast-moving."*

### AI Stack
- **Glean**: secure access to all documents and dashboards, agent built on top
- **Claude**: used extensively across Snap for building agents

### Why 2025 Is a Turning Point
- Almost entering Fortune 500
- Almost at 1 billion monthly active users
- About to launch Specs after 12 years of investment
- But: still not net income profitable (due to heavy investment in the future)

### What Needs to Be Proven

- Solid foundation for the next chapter (Specs)

### The Middle Child Problem
- Larger than Pinterest or Reddit
- Much smaller than Meta and Google
- *"We occupy this very interesting middle position in the market"*
- This year is about defining who Snap is as a company

## 💬 The Contrarian Take: Humanity Over Technology

> *"We're in an industry where so much of the conversation is focused on technology. Humanity is far more important because humanity dictates how technology is adopted."*

- Technology leaders think people will blindly adopt new technology
- *"We're going to enter a period of time where there's going to be a huge amount of societal pushback on a lot of the changes coming with AI"*
- The industry needs to put humanity first
- Make sure tools are advancing humanity's goals, not just business goals

## 🧒 Parenting and Screens

### Screen Policy by Age
- **2-year-old**: Zero screen time (exception: YouTube bobcat tractor videos during haircuts)
- **6 and 7-year-olds**: Infrequent movie watching, Mod Retro gaming devices (Palmer Luckey's startup), no phones
- **15-year-old**: All in on technology — school, friends, Snapchat

### On AI and Kids
- Teachers not yet integrating AI into curriculum much
- Eldest (Flynn) most fluent in AI tools
- Beta testers for Specs
- *"As a kid, we're all incredibly imaginative. What's so cool about AI is how in nearly an instant, you can take any idea you have and make something."*
