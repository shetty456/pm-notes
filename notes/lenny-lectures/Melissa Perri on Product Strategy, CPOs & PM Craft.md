## The Root Cause of Most Product Org Problems

### The Training Trap
- Most companies believe their problems stem from **undertrained people**
- Melissa's observation: **99% of the time it's actually goal-setting and strategy deployment**
- Training people without strategic context = people with skills but no direction
- Result: effort without impact — teams busy, metrics flat

### The "Missing Middle"
- Teams know what they're building
- Managers know what teams are doing
- **Executives have no idea how any of it connects to business goals**
- This gap — the "missing middle" — is the most common structural failure she sees
- Without it, product and tech become a "black box" to leadership

## The Scale-Up Inflection Point

### What Happens at Scale
- Early stage = **execution mode**: rapid experimentation, all hands on deck, founder/early leader in the weeds
- Scale-up = **strategy mode**: need comprehensive product strategy, executive communication, portfolio management
- The jump from **single product → multi-product** is where most companies struggle
- First time they've had to:
  - Form a truly comprehensive strategy
  - Prioritize across competing initiatives
  - Deploy that strategy to hundreds → thousands of employees
- Some companies skip early growth (clear second/third product from day one) but hit the same wall when they **plateau**

### Why It's Hard
- Leaders in these roles often **haven't done it before**
- No playbook handed to them
- Requires skills (financial modeling, board communication, org design) not needed in earlier stages
- Backlog pressure is intense — every customer wants something, prioritization feels impossible

## When & How to Hire a CPO

### Diagnostic Signals (Melissa's Field Tests)

**Executive-level signals:**
- Board/execs say *"I don't know what's happening in product/tech"*
- No one is confidently projecting roadmap → revenue to the board
- Executive team has **misaligned goals** — competing rather than collaborating
- CEO doesn't know whether they need a product leader or what one does

**Team-level signals:**
- Go to each team, ask: *"What's the most important thing you could be doing and why?"*
- Try to ladder answers up into a strategy yourself
- If it **doesn't connect** → no strategy being deployed from the top
- If PMs are **frustrated** (not confused) → they're good, but lacking direction from above
- If PMs are **confused** → possible leadership gap AND talent gap

**Hiring signals:**
- Trouble attracting strong PMs (no one to learn from)
- Lots of junior PMs with no training path or career ladder
- No consistent process for PMs to follow

### Trigger Events for CPO Hire
- ARR crossing **$20–30M** in high-growth SaaS
- Transitioning to **multi-product** strategy (2+ products)
- Entering **drastically new markets** or geographies
- Major **merger or acquisition**
- Significant **pivot or transformation**
- Portfolio complexity growing beyond what a VP can manage alone

### CPO vs. VP/Head of Product — Detailed Breakdown

**VP / Head of Product is great at:**
- Implementing processes so product runs smoothly
- Rolling up roadmaps across PMs
- Training junior PMs
- Managing 1–2 products effectively
- Functional leadership within product

**CPO additionally needs to:**
- Interface confidently with the board
- Deeply understand financials and create revenue projections from roadmap
- Be "joined at the hip" with CRO, CFO, head of sales
- Oversee multiple functions (product + design + product ops + sometimes analytics or engineering)
- Navigate executive dynamics and bring functions together
- Own the full product portfolio strategy across complex, multi-product environments

**When to stay with VP:**
- Scope is limited to product only
- No need for cross-functional executive leadership
- Company not yet at multi-product complexity

**When to move to CPO:**
- Multi-product or complex platform strategy
- Need one leader to unify product, design, ops, sometimes engineering
- Executive team needs a true peer-level product voice

### On Bringing in Consultants / Interim CPOs

**When it makes sense:**
- CEO unsure what kind of product leader they need
- Something clearly isn't working but unclear how to fix it
- Need to bridge the gap while hiring a permanent CPO

**Melissa's model with Insight Venture Partners:**
- Never in a company hands-on for **more than 3 months**
- Within that window: hire the permanent CPO, keep teams moving, set enough roadmap, implement basic processes
- Goal: hand off a company where the incoming CPO can hit the ground running — customer interviews recorded, strategy drafted, roadmap in place, key contacts identified

**Critical caveat:**
- Consultants only work **if the company is willing to take action**
- Melissa turns away companies that aren't ready for hard decisions
- Hard decisions often include: changing people, restructuring, hiring senior talent, radically revising strategy
- *"You can hire a consultant but if you don't listen to the consultant nothing's going to change"*

## Strategy: Signs, Symptoms & Solutions

### Signs of No Strategy (Detailed)

**Operational symptoms:**
- Teams working 80-hour weeks, releasing constantly, metrics not moving
- Executives describe product/tech as a "black box"
- Teams spread too thin across too many initiatives
- No single initiative has enough resources to actually move a metric

**Alignment symptoms:**
- Ask 10 people the company vision → get 10 different answers
- Executives have **different goals** and are pitted against each other
- Sales chasing net new logos in markets that don't align with product roadmap
- No one can explain how their work ladders up to company objectives

**Leadership symptoms:**
- Strategy exists only **in people's heads** — never written down
- Vision is a tagline, not a direction (e.g. *"be the backbone of healthcare"* — no one could elaborate)
- No explicit prioritization of strategic intents

### Melissa's Diagnostic Exercise
1. Go to every team → ask *"What are you working on and why?"*
2. Attempt to ladder answers up into a coherent strategy yourself
3. If you can't connect them → strategy isn't being deployed
4. Separately, ask all executives: *"What's the vision?"* and *"What does good look like?"*
5. Compare answers — divergence = misalignment at the top

### The Two-Pager Framework (Each Level of the Org)

**Level 1 — Executive (Strategic Intents):**
- Where did the company come from? How has it changed?
- External threats and competitive landscape
- How are we positioned to reach the vision?
- What are we going to do AND not do?
- Prioritized strategic intents for next 2–3 years:
  - Go upmarket / downmarket?
  - Expand geographically?
  - Enter a new market or opportunity?
  - Double down on existing customers?

**Level 2 — Director/VP (Product Initiatives):**
- Problem-oriented, not solution-oriented
- Meaty — usually made up of multiple epics
- Tied directly to one or more strategic intents
- Written by directors/VPs for their scope (not the CPO)

**Level 3 — Team (Solutions/Epics):**
- What will we actually build?
- How does it solve the problem in the initiative above?
- How does it connect to the strategic intent above that?

**Linking them:**
- All docs live in Google Docs / wiki, linked together
- Anyone should be able to read from team-level solution all the way up to executive vision
- The "strategy tree" should be navigable end-to-end

### What Good Strategy Deployment Looks Like
- Every team member can explain: *"We're building X because it solves Y for customers, which drives Z business outcome, which gets us toward [strategic intent]"*
- **Less stakeholder infighting** — executives share goals, trade-offs are objective not emotional
- Trade-off conversations sound like: *"If we do strategic intent 1, we can't do 2 — are we okay with that?"* — calm, data-driven
- PMs stop asking *"why are we building this?"* — they already know

## Product Vision — Deep Dive

### What Makes a Bad Vision
- A tagline with no elaboration (*"be the backbone of healthcare"*)
- Describes where you are **today**, not where you're going
- So vague that different people interpret it completely differently
- Doesn't say how you're **different** from competitors
- Doesn't say what you **won't** do

### What Makes a Good Vision
- **Concrete enough** to picture — people can say *"I can see how we'll get there someday"*
- **Lofty enough** that you can't achieve it by shipping one thing
- Describes a **future state** meaningfully different from today
- Articulates **who your future customers are** (not just current ones)
- Explains **how value will be different** from what you provide today
- States **why you're better** — specifically, not just "we'll be the best"
- Explicitly states **what you won't do** — this is underrated and powerful
- Addresses **how you're different from competitors** — most visions skip this entirely

### Melissa's Vision Writing Process
1. **Brain dump freely** — write down everything: competitor analysis, customer types, value differences, future state ideas
2. Ask: *"What type of value will we bring? For whom? How is it different from today?"*
3. Ask: *"How are we different from Competitor A, B, C? What could we do that they don't?"*
4. Ask: *"What are we NOT going to do?"*
5. Combine **writing + visuals** — prose for depth, presentation/diagrams for alignment
6. Bring in a designer — even rough sketches or ecosystem diagrams dramatically improve clarity and buy-in

### Real Example (Athenahealth)
- Head of UX + VP of Product collaborated on a vision presentation
- Used **mocked-up prototypes** (not tested, not final) to make the vision tangible
- Visual + written combination got the team aligned faster than words alone

## Getting Better at Strategy as a PM

### For Individual Contributors
- **Pretend you're the CPO** — what would you do if you had that scope? Work through it mentally even without the authority
- This builds the "reps" of strategic thinking before you have the role
- Ask your CPO/VP: *"How did you get to these three priorities? What did you look at?"* — understand their thought process, not just the output
- **Talk to Sales** — win/loss analysis is gold; find out why you're losing to competitors
- Talk to other departments — they have market knowledge PMs rarely access
- Get exposure to **financials** — strategy at senior levels is inseparable from revenue modeling

### For Leaders Setting Strategy
- Start with **data from everywhere**: internal analytics, financial data, customer research, market research
- Hire or partner with a **data analyst** — give them questions, let them find patterns
- Combine: internal data + customer research + company goals → informed strategic choices
- Accept that **you won't be 100% certain** — make the best informed decision, then stay willing to correct course
- Strategy = best guess in a direction + continuous reevaluation + willingness to pivot

### On Learning in General
- **Learn by doing** — execution gives you the most learning
- After execution: **retrospect** — what worked, what didn't, what do I want to get better at?
- Then **deep dive** on that specific gap (not everything at once)
- Example: Melissa noticed PMs writing thousands of user stories for tiny features → went deep on Agile origins to understand and fix the root cause
- Study **how other companies did it** — Netflix strategy evolution, etc. — everyone does it differently, but the underlying questions are the same
- **Don't be dogmatic** about any framework — if it doesn't serve you, change it
- Build your **own toolbox** through iteration

## Product Operations — Book Preview

### Why Product Ops Emerged
- At scale (e.g. 350 PMs at Athenahealth), product management breaks down in predictable ways:
  - Every team has a **different roadmap format** — can't roll up into strategy
  - **Same customers** being interviewed by multiple teams repeatedly → customer fatigue
  - **18 different meeting types** with wrong people in the room
  - PMs can't get the data they need to make informed decisions
  - No career ladders for PMs
  - Processes that work for 5 PMs collapse at 50 or 500

### The Three Pillars of Product Ops

**1. Internal Data & Insights**
- Aggregates data from: financial systems, user analytics, product metrics, OKR tracking tools
- Surfaces it in usable formats for product leaders and teams
- Helps monitor strategy progress — are we on track?
- Enables informed pivots when data shows a direction isn't working
- Think: embedded analyst function serving the product org

**2. Customer Research & User Insights (External Data)**
- Standardizes the *process* of customer research — not centralizing it, but scaling it
- Prevents the same customers from being contacted repeatedly by different teams
- Records and indexes user interviews so insights are searchable and reusable
- Handles market research subscriptions, subject matter expert access
- Goal: **empower more PMs to do research**, not gatekeep it
- Athenahealth example: discovered 10 different teams had asked the same hospital the same question — product ops fixed the coordination layer

**3. Process & Cadence Standardization**
- Owns recurring strategic touchpoints: monthly roadmap check-ins, quarterly planning sessions
- Defines: who's in the room, what's reviewed, what decisions get made, what the outputs are
- Standardizes **cross-functional formats** (e.g. how roadmap updates go to Sales, how product marketing is kept in sync)
- Does NOT standardize team-internal processes (standups, retros — teams own those)
- Key distinction: *"I don't care how a team does their standups. I do care what format your roadmap comes in."*

## Advice for PMs on Information Overload

- There is no shortage of PM content — the risk is **consuming without applying**
- Focus first on **doing the job**, then identify specific gaps
- Pick **one gap at a time** to go deep on — don't try to absorb everything
- General PM framework (talk to users → test → build → measure → iterate) is broadly agreed upon — the debate is in *how*, not *what*
- Find what works for **your context** — codify it, use it, iterate on it
- If a process or framework isn't helping you get better → **stop doing it**
- Do **retrospectives on yourself**: is this making me a better PM? Yes → keep it. No → change it.
