## 📖 What Is Strategy?

### The Core Definition
> *"A strategy is a design for overcoming a high-stakes challenge. It's a mixture of policy and action designed to deal with a challenge."*

- The word comes from Greek: *Strategos* — Athens elected 10 Strategoi to deal with issues of the day (Persian invasion, plague, temple funding)
- Strategy was never purely military — it was always about dealing with issues and challenges
- The challenge can be positive (discovered oil in the back 40) or negative (new innovation driving you out of the market)

### The David and Goliath Insight
- Strategy stories are about **discovered strength** — surprise at how someone won
- *"Look at how he got made in five moves. That's a strategy story."*
- Strategy is emotionally about: "Wow, look at how they did that. Look at how Steve Jobs changed the world when people couldn't expect it."

## 🔺 The Kernel: Three Essential Elements of Good Strategy

### Element 1: Diagnosis
> *"Diagnosis is an understanding of the situation that you're in."*

- Not just understanding the world — understanding the **challenge you face**
- Humans can't understand all of reality, so diagnosis is also a decision about what to pay attention to
- Involves hypotheses about what's going on and how things connect
- The key question: **"What makes it hard?"**
- *"If you push, he'll tell you why it's hard. But that understanding hasn't been percolated into a strategy."*

**The diagnosis trap:**
- Saying "we're not growing fast enough" is NOT a diagnosis — it's a statement of value
- *"I'd like to be taller than I am. I'd like to have more hair. You want to grow faster. Okay, so what's holding you back?"*
- A real diagnosis says "because" — not just "therefore"

### Element 2: Guiding Policy
> *"The guiding policy is what are we going to do? It's the core of the strategy — here's how we're dealing with the situation."*

- NOT a laundry list of everything you wish would happen
- Priority means **the first** — not "grab everything that might matter"
- *"You wouldn't want to be in a commercial airplane and hear the tower say, 'I'm giving priority to the following three planes on runway five.' Right away, you know there's something wrong."*
- The guiding policy can be long-term — you don't have to change strategy every five years if you're making Almond Joy candy bars

### Element 3: Coherent Actions
> *"You have to do something. And what you do has to be coherent in several ways."*

**Three types of coherence required:**
1. Actions must address the diagnosis and implement the guiding policy
2. Actions must not fight each other (no self-contradictions)
3. Actions must be focused — not diffused across too many directions

**The growth vs. profit example:**
- Most companies have strategic goals of increasing growth AND increasing profit
- *"Growth and profit, they battle each other. CEO after CEO will stand up and say, 'We're going to grow and we're going to make a profit.' This is baby talk."*

**The missing element:**
- *"The most important part of a strategy, the part that's so easy to leave out because people like to think of strategy as this high-level conceptual thing, is the coherent action."*

## ❌ Bad Strategy: The Four Signs

### 1. Mistaking Goals for Strategy
> *"Goals are the engineering of how companies work to some extent. But abstract, high-level goals — they're not strategy, they're ambitions."*

- A list of all the things you wish would happen is not strategy
- *"A set of profit goals or performance goals — that's bad strategy."*

### 2. Missing Diagnosis
- The US education example: "We're falling behind in education" → "Therefore, more people should go to college"
- *"A real diagnosis would say 'because.' We jumped immediately to 'therefore.'"*
- The 17 intelligence agencies example: "The strategy is they're going to work together more effectively" — but no diagnosis of WHY they're not working together

### 3. Fluff and Word Salad
- Using fancy words to describe the situation to make it sound more abstract and therefore more "strategic"
- *"Word salad writing that people try to use to describe their situation. It sounds more abstract, therefore it's more strategic."*

### 4. Incoherent Actions
- Doing things that obviously fight one another
- *"We're going to burn less oil and at the same time we're going to import more oil. It doesn't make any sense."*

## 🔥 The Magnifying Glass: Focus as the Source of Power

### The Summer Camp Story
> *"To burn that black thread, there has to be a source of power (the sun), there has to be a focus (the magnifying glass), and it has to be a target that can be affected (the black thread, not white thread)."*

**The three requirements for strategic action:**
1. **Source of power** — not advantage or efficiency, but power
2. **Focus** — concentrating that power
3. **Target that can actually be affected** — choosing achievable objectives

### Why Focus Is So Hard
- *"Each time you say yes, you risk turning a nascent good strategy into a bad strategy."*
- Complex organizations have different interests pursuing different things with different clientele → gradual diffusion of effort
- *"One of the big issues in strategy is simply the organization. You have a hard time focusing energy because of all the different interests."*

## ⚡ Power: The Foundation of Competitive Strategy

### What Power Is
> *"In a competitive situation, the fundamental aspect of power is something that's going to give you some sort of advantage — usually an asymmetry of some kind."*

- If two fighters are equally balanced, it's 50/50 — strategy requires exploiting an asymmetry
- Power = leverage, but also: being first to recognize something, reputation, relationships, knowledge others don't have

### Types of Power and Asymmetry

**Reputation:**
- Can be a source of power (IBM's relationships with every large corporation)
- Can also be the opposite of power (people don't expect you to do something new)

**The IBM/Gerstner Example:**
- IBM's primary asset: respected relationships with every large corporation on the planet
- Gerstner's strategy: "Bear hug our customers. We're going to serve our customers."
- But then: IBM's big company customers were the most hesitant to move to cloud → IBM became disadvantaged in the new world because it inherited the wrong orientation

**Network Effects:**
- More users → more useful product
- First arose with telephone systems
- Bill Gates and MS-DOS/BASIC: network effects catapulted him even when people were stealing the software
- Amazon: ease of shopping keeps people from leaving
- Social media: no one wants to be on a platform with only three other people
- Generative AI: size of data for training may create stronger effects than ever seen before

**The Google/Bing asymmetry:**
- Google can improve search based on all the searches happening daily
- Bing can't catch up because it doesn't have as much data to train on
- *"Unless there's some new innovation that isn't just the amount of data, the leader again has this asymmetry working for it."*

### How to Find Your Power
> *"In what way is my company different than other companies? What do we know that other people don't know? What do we possess that other people don't possess?"*

- Start with asymmetries — what makes you different?
- Sometimes you have to redefine your space down small to actually see it
- Power can be: who you can hire, your customer base, your knowledge, your relationships
- The restaurant analogy: *"I trained under a chef who's been very successful. Oh, that's interesting. Tell me more."* — that's where the asymmetry is

### Where Power Fits in Strategy
- Power is the reason your strategy makes sense — it's what makes it not an even bet
- *"When you walk into a casino, the general casino games, you're going to expect to lose. In business, what makes you think you can succeed? There has to be some asymmetry."*
- Power is derived from history: reputation, institutional skill, knowledge, resources that competitors don't have equal access to

## 🏔️ The Crux: Finding the Hardest Part

### The Mountain Climbing Origin
> *"A crux in a climb is the hardest part, the hardest pitch or the hardest move. The basic advice to a climber is if you can't do the crux, don't do the climb."*

### The Crux in Business
- The crux is the hardest part of the problem
- Skilled designers focus on what makes the problem hard, and by focusing on the difficulty, they see a way around it

**The I.M. Pei/Louvre Example:**
- Problem: needed an entrance to the Louvre without obscuring the building
- Competing needs: entrance needed, but can't block the historic architecture
- Insight: *"Build a transparent building, build a building out of glass, so that you see through to the Louvre."*
- But rectangular glass gets dirty on top → pyramid shape

**The Elon Musk/SpaceX Example:**
- Why is it so hard to reuse a rocket? It burns up coming back at 18,000 mph
- *"Why not, like science fiction, just turn it around and have it land on its rocket?"*
- *"Well, there's not enough fuel."*
- *"Well, why aren't we carrying more fuel?"*

**The Voyager Navigation Example:**
- Problem: how do you know exactly where Jupiter is (within 100 miles)?
- Insight: *"Take a picture of it once we're halfway there. We'll send that picture back and we'll see it against the distant stars. Now we'll have a triangulation."*

### The Nature of Insight
> *"Insight is not magical. It comes from immersing yourself in the nature of the problem. And you will have, at some point, an insight about how to deal with it."*

- Doesn't always come when you want it — can come while driving, in the shower, the next day
- Charles Darwin's insight into evolution came as he stepped off a carriage into a village green
- *"Strategy isn't picking a strategy out of a list of common strategies. It's looking at the problem, what makes that problem hard, and seeing a way to solve it."*

## 📋 The Action Agenda: A Better Name for Strategy

### Why "Action Agenda" Is Better Than "Strategy"
> *"Don't call it a strategy, call it an action agenda. You're not creating a strategy, you're creating an action agenda. What are we going to do about this problem?"*

- The concept of strategy has been beaten up by hundreds of books and thousands of websites
- People get drawn in different directions by all the competing advice
- *"It's not five years out and 10 years out. It's not your general mission to build a better world."*

### The Ambitions → Challenges → Action Framework

**Step 1: Start with ambitions**
- Everyone has ambitions, every company should have ambitions
- Richard's personal example at 25: top business school professor, Fortune director, Morgan Drophead Plus 4, climb great mountains, fly aircraft, beautiful wife, Paris townhouse

**Step 2: Filter by what's achievable**
- *"Which of your ambitions can you begin to make progress towards reaching, and what's holding you back?"*
- You're choosing a challenge — you can't do them all
- The challenge has to be: (A) important, and (B) achievable/addressable

**Step 3: Identify the action steps**
- *"Okay, we're going to go after this and here's the action steps we're going to take to do that."*
- These are things we're going to DO — not goals we're going to achieve
- Big company: action steps may extend 2-3 years; smaller company: 6 months to a year

### The Mission/Vision/Values Trap
> *"There's huge numbers of people out there willing to sell you advice on how your mission, and your vision, and your values, and all these things that have to be in place before you can have a strategy. That's not true."*

- Mission, vision, values are a different literature form
- They're not prerequisites for strategy
- *"Begin to try to identify the one or two key challenges that can actually be addressed and what are we going to do about it?"*

## 🏢 Organizational Dynamics: The Biggest Hindrance

### Why Organizations Struggle with Strategy

**Diversity of interests:**
- Every piece of information comes with an agenda — sometimes obvious, sometimes hidden
- Rumsfeld quote: *"I've got people here who are experts on anything. But they all disagree. And each have their own private agenda — a contract they want to get, a company they want to support, a conceptual idea they want to push."*

**Fear of action:**
- Action involves people changing what they do
- It involves changing power relationships — someone who was alpha may not be alpha anymore
- *"CEOs are hesitant to do that. More so today than when I was a young man."*

**The leadership literature problem:**
> *"Most of the management section in bookstores is about leadership. And the leadership is mostly about perfecting yourself. The theory is that if you perfect yourself, somehow rays come out of your head and people will follow you."*

- Transformational leadership theory has percolated through the system
- *"You have to tell people at some point, 'We're going to do this, and we're going to do it this way, and Bob's going to be in charge of this aspect.'"*
- There's a hesitancy to make those choices — but that's part of the action agenda

### The Nokia Case Study
- Nokia was the leading phone maker in the world, then lost its ability to compete
- Two key failures:
  1. Replaced engineers who ran the company with lawyers and accountants — lost feel for hardware/software issues
  2. Put in a matrix organization that so diffused power that nobody was actually in charge of anything
- *"The CEO kept pounding the desk saying, 'Apple's coming out with a smartphone and you have a touchscreen, and somebody here should make one of those.' But there was no one in authority to do such a thing."*

### The Solution: Hierarchy and Decisiveness
- *"Typically, we need hierarchy of power, because there has to be some mechanism for deciding what we're going to do."*
- Someone has to say: "It's going to be this way."
- The Woodrow Wilson example: Secretary of State disagreed and quit — that kind of decisiveness is rare today

## 🔍 The Importance of History and Diagnosis

### Why History Matters for Strategy
> *"If you don't have access to other times and other places and other things that have happened, it's very hard to think strategically about the situation you're in because there isn't a science of strategy."*

- Strategy is not like physics or engineering — you can't write equations
- A lot of it is based on analogy to previous human experience
- *"The best histories are written by the people at the moment it's happening or very soon after."*

### The Great Depression Exercise
- Go online and look at the Wall Street Journal or New York Times from September 1928
- Read the front page every day and watch it unfold
- *"Ford just laid off the people, but it's temporary. They'll hire them back."*
- Nobody understood what was happening — even today, economists disagree on why it happened
- Richard's own hypothesis: the adoption curve for capital goods (like cars) peaked in 1929 — everyone who could afford one had one

### The Diagnosis Skill
> *"The most important intellectual tool we have is to think again. To say, okay, I came up with this diagnosis because I thought this. Is there another way to look at this situation? Is there a bigger piece of wood over there than I can grab onto?"*

- The falling-off-a-boat analogy: you grab the first piece of wood you see (first diagnosis), but is it the right one?
- The danger: once you have a diagnosis, it feels like relief — you stop looking for a better one

## 🚀 Strategy for Startups

### The Nature of the Startup Bet
> *"You're making a bet. You're making a bet like an oil well driller. There's a certain amount of bet that's going on, and you should be clear about the nature of the bet."*

- Reality is revealed in bits and pieces as time passes
- Research on Silicon Valley startups: they start aiming at a particular product-market solution, and generally it doesn't work
- The ones that survive and prosper **switch** — different customer, slightly different product — until something clicks

### The Double-Minded Founder
> *"You've got to be of two minds. You've got to be convinced of the certainty that you're going to win, and you've got to be willing to shift when things aren't working."*

- *"It's almost a human skill to both commit and to be willing to move."*
- *"He who forecasts the future lies, even if he tells the truth." — Arab saying*

### The Action Agenda for Startups
- Quick adaptation to changing conditions
- The truffle hound analogy: searching until something clicks, then growing and adding functions and assets

### Value Denial: Finding Startup Opportunities
> *"What is it that you should be able to buy but you can't?"*

- Lost baggage: why can't you buy insurance that guarantees your baggage arrives?
- Home remodeling: can't find someone who will be on time
- Hong Kong airport: you can drop luggage off downtown and it arrives checked in — why not in the US?
- *"Look around you, look at everything you've got and say, 'How shouldn't it be?' And there's opportunities there."*

### The Perfect Window Exercise
- What should a window do?
  - Let in light (but not always — sometimes you want to watch TV)
  - Let in air (but not bugs — screen)
  - Let in air (but not noise — harder)
  - Have shutters or not
- *"We don't have a perfect window, but think about it — windows could be better than they are."*
- This simple exercise leads to new businesses if you pursue it

## 🔧 The Foundry: Richard's Strategy Process

### What a Foundry Is
- 2-4 days off-site with the CEO plus 7-8 senior people
- Richard facilitates — doesn't tell them what to do, urges them to look more deeply
- Goal: produce an action agenda

### The Process
1. Get 25-50 problems/challenges up on the board (Post-It notes)
2. People realize: *"I haven't ever done this before."* When they see 25 different problems, they realize they can't do them all
3. Begin to ask: which ones are really important AND really addressable?
4. Identify the 1-2 key challenges that can actually be addressed
5. Determine coherent actions to take on those challenges

### The Outcome
- *"From time to time, after the foundries, they all say to me, 'Well, where's the strategy? Where's the document with the mission, vision, all that?' No, that's not what we do. We're doing an action agenda."*
- Most foundries help the company gather its wits and resources and actually do something
