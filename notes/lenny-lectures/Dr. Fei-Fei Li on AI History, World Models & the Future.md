## 📚 The History of AI: From Winter to Revolution

### The Founding Era (1950s–1980s)
- **Alan Turing** (1940s): Asked "Can machines think?" — proposed the conversational chatbot test
- **1956 Dartmouth Workshop**: Professor John McCarthy coined "artificial intelligence"
- **1950s–1980s**: Logic systems, expert systems, early neural network exploration
- These were the founding fathers of the field

### The Machine Learning Era (Late 1980s–Early 2000s)
- Marriage between computer programming and statistical learning
- Key insight: purely rule-based programs can't account for the vast range of cognitive capabilities
- Machines need to **learn patterns** — not just follow rules
- The cat recognition example: if you show a machine 3 cats, the goal is for it to recognize the 4th, 5th, 6th cat — that's learning

### The AI Winter
- Late 1990s through early 2000s: little public interest, not much funding
- But ideas were still flowing — researchers kept working
- Fei-Fei entered the field in 2000 at Caltech — *"smack in the middle of the AI winter"*

### The ImageNet Breakthrough (2006–2012)
- Fei-Fei chose to focus on **visual intelligence** — humans are deeply visual animals
- North star problem: **object recognition** — the building block of perceptual understanding
- Key insight: *"Human learning as well as evolution is actually a big data learning process"*
- Humans learn with constant experience; evolution works through experiencing the world
- The critically overlooked ingredient: **big data**

### Building ImageNet (2006–2007)
- Ambitious goal: curate the entire internet's image data on objects
- Result: **15 million images**, **22,000 concepts**
- Used WordNet (linguist work on word taxonomy) as the conceptual framework
- Open-sourced to the research community
- Held annual ImageNet Challenge to encourage participation

### The 2012 Moment: Birth of Modern AI
- Toronto researchers led by **Professor Jeff Hinton** participated in ImageNet Challenge
- Used ImageNet big data + **two Nvidia GPUs** (gaming GPUs)
- Created the first neural network algorithm that made huge progress on object recognition
- *"That combination of the trio technology — big data, neural network, and GPU — was the golden recipe for modern AI"*

### The Three Ingredients That Still Power AI Today
1. **Big data** (now internet-scale text instead of images)
2. **Neural networks** (now much more complex architectures like transformers)
3. **GPUs** (now hundreds of thousands instead of two)

> *"If you look at the ingredients of what brought ChatGPT to the world technically, it still uses these three ingredients."*

## 🤖 On AGI: A Scientist's Perspective

### AGI Is More a Marketing Term Than a Scientific Term
> *"I don't know if anyone has ever defined AGI. As a scientist, I take science very seriously."*

- Many definitions: from "superpower for machines" to "machines that can become economically viable agents"
- Fei-Fei's view: the founding question was always "can machines think and do things the way humans can" — that's the north star of AI
- *"I feel AGI is more a marketing term than a scientific term"*
- Alan Turing asked the same question in the 1940s — the distinction between AI and AGI might not be meaningful

### What AI Still Cannot Do
- Count chairs in a video of office rooms (something a toddler can do)
- Derive equations like Newton did from observing celestial bodies
- Exhibit the level of creativity, extrapolation, and abstraction that produced Newton's laws
- Provide the emotional cognitive intelligence of a teacher-student conversation about motivation and passion

### The Demis Hassabis Test
- Give the most cutting-edge model all information until end of 20th century → can it produce Einstein's breakthroughs?
- Answer: No
- Even harder version: Give AI all modern instrument data of celestial bodies (which Newton didn't have) → can it derive Newton's 17th century laws of bodily movement?
- *"Today's AI cannot do that"*

### Do We Need More Breakthroughs?
> *"There is not a single deeply scientific discipline in human history that has arrived at a place that says we're done innovating. AI is one of the youngest disciplines in human civilization."*

- Scaling laws (more data, more GPUs, bigger models) still have room to run
- But absolutely need more innovations beyond scaling
- *"We're still scratching the surface"*

## 🌐 World Models: The Next Frontier

### Why Spatial Intelligence Matters
- Humans are deeply visual, spatial animals — not just language animals
- Think about a chaotic first responder scene: fire, traffic accident, natural disaster
- *"A lot of that is movements, spontaneous understanding of objects, worlds, situational awareness. Language is part of that. But a lot of those situations language cannot get you to put down the fire."*
- The lynchpin connecting embodied AI (robotics), visual intelligence, and language: **spatial intelligence**

### The DNA Discovery Example
- Rosalind Franklin's X-ray diffraction photo: a flat 2D image of a cross-like structure
- Watson and Crick used this 2D photo to reason in 3D space and deduce the double helix
- *"You cannot think in 2D and deduce that structure. You have to think in 3D spatial intelligence."*
- Even scientific discovery requires spatial intelligence

### What Is a World Model?
> *"A model that can allow anyone to create any worlds in their mind's eye by prompting — whether it's an image or a sentence — and also be able to interact in this world."*

Three capabilities:
1. **Create**: Generate worlds from prompts (image, sentence, multiple images)
2. **Interact**: Browse, walk, pick up objects, change things
3. **Reason**: Plan paths, understand spatial relationships, enable robot navigation

### How World Models Differ from Video Generation
- Video generation: passive, flat 2D output — you watch it, then it's done
- World models: genuinely 3D structure, navigable, interactive
- Plato's Allegory of the Cave: vision is about making sense of the 3D/4D world from 2D inputs
- *"Spatial intelligence to me is deeper than creating that flat 2D world"*

### The Big Applications
1. **Robotics**: Key missing piece for embodied AI — robots need to understand and act in 3D worlds
2. **Games**: Infinitely playable, invented from your imagination
3. **Creativity and design**: Buildings, machines, homes, environments
4. **Scientific discovery**: Spatial reasoning for research
5. **Human augmentation**: Just as language models augment language tasks, world models augment spatial tasks

## 🔮 The Bitter Lesson and Why It's Not Enough for Robotics

### What Is the Bitter Lesson?
- Paper by Richard Sutton (Turing Award winner)
- Observation: simpler models with a ton of data always win over complex models with less data
- Fei-Fei: *"That was not bitter for me — it was a sweet lesson. That's why I built ImageNet."*

### Why the Bitter Lesson Alone Won't Work for Robotics

**Problem 1: Data alignment mismatch**
- Language models: training data = words/tokens → output = words/tokens → **perfect alignment**
- Robotics: you want **actions in 3D worlds** but training data (web videos) **lacks actions in 3D worlds**
- *"You have to find different ways to fit a square in a round hole"*
- Solutions being explored: teleoperation data, synthetic data — but still early

**Problem 2: Robots are physical systems**
- Robots are closer to self-driving cars than to language models
- Need: brains + physical body + application scenarios
- Self-driving cars: 20 years from Stanford's DARPA Challenge win (2005) to Waymo on SF streets — and still not done
- Self-driving cars are simpler: metal boxes on 2D surfaces, goal is NOT to touch anything
- Robots: 3D things in 3D world, goal IS to touch things → much harder

**The Hope**
- World models will unlock a lot of information for robots
- Deep learning is accelerating the "brains" component
- But the full journey (hardware, use cases, supply chains) will take time

## 🎮 Marble: World Labs' First Product

### What Is Marble?
- The world's first **large world model** product
- Built on World Labs' frontier models (1+ year of development)
- **"Prompt to worlds"**: describe a scene with text or images → get a navigable 3D world
- Can be experienced in VR goggles — you can walk around inside it
- Available at: **marble.worldlabs.ai**

### The Team Behind It
- Four co-founders: Fei-Fei Li, Justin Johnson, Kristoff Lassner, Ben Mildenhall
- All from research backgrounds: AI, computer graphics, computer vision
- Team of ~30 people — predominantly researchers and research engineers
- *"We really believe that we want to create a company anchored in deep tech but building serious products"*

### The Dot Visualization Feature
- When entering a world, you see dots before textures render
- This was an **intentional design feature** — not part of the model
- Engineers worked on multiple versions to guide users into the world
- Users love it — many describe it as delightful
- Fei-Fei: *"It was really satisfying to hear that this intentional visualization feature has delighted our users"*
- Lenny's reaction: *"It makes me think about the Matrix"*

### Current Use Cases

| Use Case | Details |
|---|---|
| **Virtual production/VFX** | Collaborated with Sony — cut production time by 40x on launch video |
| **Game development** | Users taking mesh exports and building VR/regular games |
| **Robotic simulation** | Creating diverse synthetic environments for robot training |
| **Psychology research** | Psychologist team studying brain responses to different immersive scenes (messy vs. clean environments) |
| **Exposure therapy** | Potential for treating phobias (heights, spiders, snakes) |
| **Design** | Architecture, product design, environment creation |

### The Unexpected Discovery Pattern
> *"This is very much the way things work in AI — put things out there early as soon as you can to discover where the big use cases are."*

- Head of ChatGPT scanned TikTok to see how people were using it
- Marble: psychologist team calling about psychiatric research was completely unexpected

## 💼 Career Lessons: How Fei-Fei Got to the Center of Everything

### The Through-Line: Intellectual Fearlessness
> *"I'm an intellectually very fearless person. And I have to say when I hire young people, I look for that."*

- When you want to make a difference, you're creating something new — you have to allow yourself to be fearless and courageous
- *"I didn't spend too much time thinking about all the failure cases"*

### Key Career Decisions
- **Princeton → Stanford**: Restarted her tenure clock to be around amazing people and the Silicon Valley ecosystem
- **Became first female director of SAIL**: Was a relatively young faculty member — wanted to make a difference for the community
- **Stanford → Google Cloud**: Wanted to work with Jeff Dean, Jeff Hinton, and other incredible people
- **Google → World Labs**: Passion + belief that people with the same mission can do incredible things

### Advice for Young Talent in AI
> *"Focus on what's important. Where's your passion? Do you align with the mission? Do you believe and have faith in this team?"*

- Many young people today over-analyze every dimension of a job decision
- The most important questions: passion alignment, mission alignment, team quality
- *"Focus on the impact you can make and the kind of work and team you can work with"*
- Don't let FOMO drive career decisions

Fei-Fei's answer: **Resounding yes — everybody has a role in AI**
