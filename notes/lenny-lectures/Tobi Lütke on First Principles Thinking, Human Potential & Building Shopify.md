## 🌪️ The Tobi Tornado

### What It Is
> *"A whole lot of change management or a conversation or conflict or real talk compressed into a very short timeframe."*

- Sees something that isn't right → has a conversation → updates priors → project might be stopped
- The team currently on the project becomes the founders of the next version, built differently
- Can feel whiplashy, but people ultimately appreciate it because **what is best ends up mattering a lot**

### The Philosophy Behind It
> *"Once I imagine something might be not the right thing to work on, I'm either incorrect — at which point this is super important that I understand why — or I'm correct — at which point it's super unfair to let people work on something that isn't going to make it."*

- A third option (ignoring it) is an abdication of CEO/founder responsibility — not a valid path
- Compressing time is critical: careers are not that long (~40 years in industry)
- Goal: at the end of your career, look back and say "we shipped incredible things we were proud of"

## 🚀 Maximizing Human Potential

### The Core Belief
> *"I really, really, really think that there is not a single person on this planet who is even close to being at their maximum potential."*

- Everyone is way, way better than they think
- The reasons we don't perform at maximum: undiscovered approaches, environments that narrow focus to unambitious things
- *"When you're unambitious, you get stuck competing with literally everyone else in the world because everyone's unambitious"*

### How Tobi Applies This
- Has a history of being right about people's potential more than they are themselves
- Harder on himself than on anyone else — believes he has way more potential than he brings to bear
- Holding people to a high standard over long careers causes them to accomplish things they didn't imagine possible
- *"Reminding people of their own potential constantly is actually a wonderful thing to do"*

### The Product Manifestation
- Shopify's product IS this philosophy: cause people to be more successful than they thought they could be
- Make merchants more ambitious about what they're building than they initially set out to do
- *"Something like this happened to me — I started a snowboard store. I didn't set out to build Shopify."*

### The School Analogy
- School optimizes for marks (a proxy metric) → overfitting → kids cheating to get marks
- The right loss function for children: **maintaining curiosity**
- *"Everyone's born extremely curious and school has a habit of getting it out of kids"*
- School fine-tunes the foundation model of a child and loses the neurons of curiosity because meandering into other topics is discouraged
- There is no speed limit for personal growth

## 📊 Why Shopify Doesn't Use OKRs

### The Goodhart's Law Problem
> *"Any metric that becomes a goal ceases to be a good metric."*

- This is the same as overfitting in machine learning — just different terminology
- No metric by itself is a complete heuristic for a complex business
- Businesses have millions of tensions that can't all be kept in harmony by optimizing for one fix

### What They Do Instead
- Extremely data-informed: invested enormous amounts in systems that give every constituent atomic bit at their fingertips
- But: decisions are made by pilots, not by the cockpit instruments
- *"The decisions are still made by pilots and we think this leads to better results"*

### The 80/20 Rule of Value
> *"The overlap of most valuable things you can do with a product and things that happen to be fully quantifiable is maybe 20%, which leaves 80% of a value space unaddressable by people who will only look at quantifiable things."*

- Shopify is comfortable with unquantifiable things: taste, quality, passion, love, hate
- The deep satisfaction a craftsperson feels when they've done a job well is actually a better proxy than whether a unit test passes

### The Most Powerful Unquantifiable Things
> *"The most powerful unquantifiable things in the world of business are fun and delight."*

- *"If all the metrics are pointing down but everyone says, 'My God, I'm having so much more fun,' I think the very next thing that will happen with some time delay is all metrics will start going up."*
- Fun is upstream from (downstream from?) so many other things
- If that doesn't happen, then you adjust course

### Why This Requires Removing OKRs
- If you want to hold the unquantifiable as stable things that people actually defer to, you need to make certain edits to the business
- OKRs remind everyone too much of companies where the only way to get promoted is by driving a metric up
- *"Shopify doesn't do OKRs or doesn't do metrics" sounds like a fortune cookie — but it's actually because metrics take you to a loss function where you often defer to less quantifiable things*

## 🔬 First Principles Thinking: The Tobi Algorithm

### The Energy Source
> *"My energy source is dissatisfaction with status quo."*

- Today is the dystopia of the future — no one who really thinks about this would want to be born 20 years before today
- Every solution, every product that exists is **highly path-dependent** — often based on compromises that were true when the decision was made but are no longer true
- The Chomsky linguistics example: we thought we needed to research the structure of grammar to make machines engage with language. Then autoregressive models showed we could just train on the internet.

### The Core Principle
> *"If you want to do something better than what exists, you have to do it differently. That is axiomatically not possible to do otherwise. It fails Archimedean logic."*

- Not an opinion — it's an axiom
- *"If most people are doing it a certain way, I by default don't want to do it that way"*
- *"If you want to do something world-class, you can't do it like everyone else"*

### The Algorithm (Tobi's Description)
**Step 1: Analyze path dependence**
- What assumptions were true when the existing solution was built that are no longer true?
- What is the existing solution overfit for?

**Step 2: Re-derive from atomic building blocks**
> *"How would we solve this problem given every fundamental building block that we have available right now?"*

- Requires understanding the power and composability of all building blocks that exist today
- Sometimes you derive exactly what everyone else does — and that's fine, it means there was wisdom encoded in the status quo

**Step 3: The thermostat function**
- Re-run the entire function over the updated state with higher fidelity information
- Would you come to the very same conclusion?
- Sometimes early in the tree of foundational assumptions, a change is made that takes you to a completely different landing zone

**Step 4: Don't skip the exercise**
> *"What isn't okay is skipping the exercise and doing the same thing everyone else does because that is an abdication of product leadership."*

### The COVID/Remote Work Example
- Shopify was a very in-person company with great collaborative spaces
- The Boolean "are people allowed to leave a house?" flipped from yes to no
- When that foundational assumption changes, the entire decision tree moves to a completely different place
- *"Don't port the office online. Let's port the internet into a company."*
- Also: Shopify was already in 4-5 cities; Tobi was having 10 hours of meetings per day with no one physically present
- COVID also meant needing to staff up rapidly → hiring everywhere became optimal
- *"Once that decision flipped, you can see how this is actually now super easy to say, 'We are going to remote'"*

### The Danger of Only Making Good Choices
> *"Most of the time you end up in a bad part of a tree, in a local maxima of a path-dependent environment, by only making good choices."*

- People think making a good choice inoculates you from mistakes — incorrect
- People think the presence of a downside disqualifies an idea — also incorrect

## 🎯 How to Disagree with Tobi

### The Invitation
> *"I immediately love it, honestly. I really crave it."*

- Disagreement requires courage — and courage is rarer than high IQ or even genius
- Disagreeing makes Tobi immediately trust the person more
- Shows they'll do what they think is right rather than what's convenient

### The Process
1. Stop immediately and say "Cool, let's figure out why there's disagreement"
2. Look for the divergence point — the unstated foundational assumption
3. *"What is our divergence point? Because you might be right."*
4. Sometimes it's an incorrect foundational assumption Tobi holds (e.g., something about Sarbanes-Oxley)

### Playing Devil's Advocate
- If everyone agrees on something, Tobi will actively play devil's advocate
- Especially if a proposal is something he could have predicted before the meeting
- *"I'm going to say this is just not that good. I think we could do way better."*
- Forces people to argue more in-depth for the veracity of their decisions

### The Keynes Principle
> *"When the facts change, I change my opinion. What do you do, sir?"*

- Consistency is overrated — flip-flopping when facts change is the correct behavior
- Holds opinions very strongly until convinced they're not correct

## ♟️ The Chess Analogy: Positional vs. Tactical Game

### Two Games in Business
**Positional game:**
- What territory on the map are you taking?
- What role do you play?
- How much trust do you have with customers?
- Are you the kind of thing they're trying to optimize out of their software spend, or the one they ask to subsume all other software spend?
- How well do pieces fit together?

**Tactical game:**
- A/B tests, conversion optimization, color changes
- *"The business world only talks about tactics"*

### The Critical Insight
> *"The sum total of all the value of a potential tactics that you could employ stays with you if you are actually doing the positional game."*

- You need to be good enough at tactics to not go out of business (don't get margin calls)
- But if you do tactics too much, you extract the entirety of the value you've created through the positional game
- *"Those are the companies that got tacticked out of their position"*

### The Monetization Cockpit
> *"The best companies resemble some kind of cockpit or a room full of dials or levers for monetization. My job is adding as many of these levers as possible to the room, and then not pulling any of them."*

- *"On a long enough timeline, playing positive-sum games with your customers is the ultimate growth hack"*
- When investment bankers tell Tobi he has enormous pricing power: *"Yeah, but I'd leave Shopify if that happens to me, so how is that good?"*

## 🤝 The Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma

### The Shopify-Stripe Partnership
- Both were very small companies when they decided to work together
- Assumption: both of us are going to win our markets, let's work together
- *"Being a good partner in business is like this corporate marshmallow test that companies tend to fail in a very funny way"*

### The Marshmallow Test for Companies
- Smart kids turn the chair around, look away from the marshmallow, sit on their hands
- Most CEOs and companies can't do this — they engage in pulling future profits forward at a discount
- *"If you're talking about long timeframes like 100 years, there is no question — the correct way to play iterated prisoner's dilemma is coordinate for both sides"*

### The Long-Term Horizon Advantage
- Long time horizon means: don't take the discount
- Customers notice when you're going into value extraction mode
- *"We are on the same side of the table as our customers"*

## 🏪 Shopify's Mission: Making Entrepreneurship More Common

### The Core Insight (Tobi's Favorite Discovery)
> *"Every single time we make a complex thing simpler, more businesses will exist on a platform."*

- Entrepreneurs are often unsophisticated, scared, don't know what to do
- The amount of people who tell you NOT to start a business is stunning
- When software stuns someone with complexity (APIs, tax configuration), on the wrong day they close the browser and say "fuck it, I'm not cut out for this"
- Shopify's answer: just make taxes correct automatically — no configuration screen needed

### The Moral Obligation
> *"When software goes bad, it makes people feel dumb. Machines do not get to negatively influence people in my mind."*

- Software is a tool wielded by people to be more powerful at things they're already great at
- Making complex things simpler is almost a moral obligation

### Why Shopify Doesn't Want Credit
> *"Shopify is a company that pushes from behind. We don't want to be written into the story."*

- Wanting to front it all leads to building a marketplace where everyone leases a component at prices that correspond to exactly your margin
- Instead: help people build their own things

## 🧬 The Talent Stack

### The Core Idea
> *"I am getting curious about some absolutely random thing and like magic it becomes the way that ends up allowing me to make a very important choice a year later."*

- Following curiosity leads to hidden harmonies — things that are clearly enduring correct insights
- Overfitting, Goodhart's law, and school's metric problem are all the same thing in different domains

### The Career Advice
- *"We are products. We are engaged in entrepreneurship. We are basically talent as a service."*
- Employment is a double opt-in subscription: "I can do these things and will be of significantly more value than you spend on me"
- Find the thing where you are in a tiny space of 5 intersections where you know more than everyone else
- *"Your career is not based on mentors you find or getting a promotion. It's about what are you too good to ignore in?"*

### The Pirate Gold Poker Set Example
- Found a pirate-themed poker set while on vacation
- Intersection of: online Texas Hold'em, quality poker set, pirate theme
- *"Enthusiasm is actually the best marketing"*
- Better to make amazing things for some people than make something everyone would tolerate

### The Ruby/JIT Compiler Example
- Shopify uses Ruby, which wasn't fast enough
- Found people who got PhDs in dynamic language just-in-time compilation
- They're having a delightful time; Shopify is now very fast
- Merged it back into Ruby Core — everyone benefits
- *"Everyone gets to profit from us needing this"*

## 💡 Final Advice for Product Leaders

> *"Every product in the world, the quality at the end of the day is simply a reflection of how much the people who created it gave a shit about the product."*

**Two things product leaders must do:**

**1. Look around corners**
- Understand the thing being done better than everyone else
- Query the entire team as a resource to determine what's around the corners

**2. Be exothermically infectious**
> *"You've got to be exothermically infectious with actually caring about this thing because just that one thing alone will make a 10 times better product."*

- Don't engage in product work on products you don't care about
- You cannot produce what the person giving you the task is looking for
- *"It isn't about the brief document, it isn't about aligning stakeholders"*
