## 🔺 The Proven Better New Framework

### The Core Philosophy
> *"Your instincts are right 95% of the time. Your ideas are wrong 75% of the time — or at best right 25% of the time."*

The framework exists to isolate your innovation zone, test many ideas around it, and fail for the right reasons rather than the wrong ones.

### The Three Components

**PROVEN**
- What is already working on this platform, for this audience, for this experience?
- Must be platform-specific: *"If it's not on this platform, it doesn't count as proven"*
- Get your PhD in proven first — become the world's leading expert on best-of-breed existing products
- *"We haven't earned the right to innovate on the camera until we are the world's leading PhD on the best mobile cameras that already exist"*
- The Sid Meier cautionary tale: The godfather of game design launched a social civilization game on Facebook — dead on arrival because his first-time user experience had too many clicks. He didn't copy the proven onboarding that even junior Zynga PMs knew cold. His innovation never got seen.

**BETTER**
- NOT what you think is better — that's called "new"
- Better = something that **10 out of 10 existing users** of that product would say "f*** yeah" to
- Usually very small increments: polish, accessibility, things power users notice most
- Often: it's now free, there's no download, something statistically demonstrable
- *"What we think is better is new. We usually are too ambitious on the new."*
- Craig Newmark example: spent two years adding photos to Craigslist listings — because he deeply understood that moving where text appeared could break the experience for people who relied on pattern recognition to scan listings quickly

**NEW**
- The back-of-the-box idea that gets people to download and try
- Accept upfront that the new idea is probably going to fail
- *"We have to accept that that new idea is probably going to fail. It's a reason to try it, but it's probably going to fail."*
- Words with Friends example: Scrabble was proven, mobile polish was better, social (Facebook graph friends already there to play) was new
- Have 4-5 new ideas ready to test — if you start with the premise that new is probably wrong, you prosecute it differently

### The Time Machine Thought Experiment
> *"What if I could go back to Mark in 2003 and say, 'Dude, you don't even realize how right you are. One of your instincts is going to be a $1.6 trillion company once, and you are a year ahead of them.'"*

- Tribe → Reddit (the "Tribes" idea became an entire industry)
- The lesson: if you're doing proven better new, you're also looking around for heat that's proven in the market that you can test

### The Moral Arbitrage of Copying
> *"In the Peter Thiel sense, it's almost a moral arbitrage. You became a founder because you wanted to be an innovator, and it can feel like a beatdown that your path to innovation starts with copying."*

- We were taught in school that copying is cheating → built moral resistance
- But this resistance makes the opportunity more available to people with less ego
- *"If you're truly ambitious, burn your resume."*
- *"Define your ambition in the eyes of your consumer, not your peers. You're not trying to win awards from your peers. You're trying to win the hearts and minds of nurses in Indiana like for Farmville."*
- If all you did was copy, there's no reason for her to choose your product. But if you took something she loves and made it one inch better, she might love that more than something she's never seen before and didn't know she wanted.

### Proven Better New in Practice: OMG Pop / Draw Something
- OMG Pop tried something totally innovative → failed completely → on their last dollar
- Ruthlessly, desperately needed a hit
- Did Proven Better New: took a proven game, perfectly copied Zynga's turn-based system from Words with Friends, made a massive hit
- Became #1 game and app in the App Store for 60 days in 2012
- Zynga acquired them

### Nikita / TBH Example
- Found something proven buried inside an Arabic-only version of a product
- *"When we can see something that is proven already in someone else's product but they have the wrong idea around it — that's gold"*

### Freeloader Origin Story
- Found offline browsing buried as a power user feature in Netscape and Internet Explorer
- Made an entire product just around that one feature
- Bandwidth was so slow that offline browsing was the killer feature — they just hadn't surfaced it

## 🎯 Be Less Ambitious to Build More Ambitious Things

### The Paradox
> *"The more ambitious you are, the more humble you should be and the smaller place you should be willing to start."*

- Facebook started as an app to check out girls and guys at Harvard
- Zynga started as a poker game on Facebook — people thought Mark had no dignity
- *"I was 41, a multi-time successful founder that could go do something important in the world. And what did I do? I made a poker game."*

### The Career Wave Pattern
1. Start humble → get product-market fit → bigger success than imagined
2. Feel like you can do anything → try something bigger and more ambitious
3. Fail because you were too ambitious and didn't pick one use case
4. Get humbled → go back to something embarrassingly small
5. Succeed again

### The Tribe Failure
- Saw social networking, saw how big the opportunity was
- Tried to do everything with Tribe — too ambitious
- Multiple things inside Tribe worked (the "urban tribes" idea), but didn't pick one
- Failed

### The Startup Advantage
> *"A Zuck needs to go really big because the revenue is already so high — anything they take on needs to be a many-billion-dollar business opportunity. As a startup, you don't need to do that. This is how you end up discovering some of the biggest ideas in the end."*

- Being willing to pursue little threads when they're flaky, when they're not yet a business
- Bolt.new example: toiled in obscurity, open-sourced it, realized combining web stacks with AI coding co-pilot made something better than anything else
- Slack example: Stuart kept trying to start game companies → humbled → found the little thing engineers used → built a product around that

## ☠️ Kill Hope Before Hope Kills You

### Hope vs. Belief
> *"Hope is confidence without basis. Hope is a prayer, but it's not founded in your lived experience with your product, your experience with people experiencing your product, and the data."*

- Too many founders and teams keep going with hope that the next release will do something magical
- The best product makers are **collecting winnings, not making bets** — they already know they have a hit before they launch

### MVP vs. Maximum Launchable Product
- "Viable" is where hope comes from — if it's viable, it might make it
- There's a difference between:
  - Putting a product out to **learn** from
  - Putting a product out that you **believe** (not hope) is going to be a hit
- *"AI is a dangerous drug because it means we can get to a viable product in much less time. I thought people would use AI to build incredible testing machines. Instead, people are using AI to build one idea in three months rather than a hundred ideas in a day."*

### Build It Wrong Before You Know It's Right
> *"Build it completely wrong before we know it's the right product. If we believe it's the wrong product, what are you going to do differently? You are not going to waste three months building the wrong product. You'd rather waste a day or a week."*

- The Farmville expansion pack story:
  - Team had $10M ad budget to run ads before launch
  - Mark: "You have 25-30 million people a day using your product — why are you putting ads somewhere else?"
  - Instead: put different art forms of the English countryside locked on the game board
  - Tested which variants got the most clicks → "coming soon, click here for early access"
  - Unintended consequence: sold $19M worth of keys for early access
  - Turned afterthought advertising into signal, direction, AND revenue

### How to Know If Your Product Is a B+
> *"If you're asking whether or not your product is an A, it's not an A."*

- Dating analogy: when you're with the right person, you know it — you're not asking "could this be the one?"
- *"Osho said if you're torn between two paths, they're both wrong."*
- When you have lightning in a bottle, everything works: you love your product, you're addicted to it, you show it to friends and they love it, metrics show it works
- Did we have to ask if GPT was it? No — we were living on it and dreaming of new things to do

### The Earth Project
- Mark pulled the plug on his metaverse project for the fourth time after 4 years and $25 million
- Was too ambitious, not going for a small idea
- *"In the last two weeks since then, I've been more inspired around ideas than I have been in four years."*
- *"There is power in pulling the plug on a B+ and even more on a D."*

## 📱 Consumer Social: The Cocktail Party Framework

### The State of Social Today
> *"We have beyond a latent demand for social. It's lost the adrenaline."*

- People are on Snapchat, Instagram, TikTok — but it feels like eating potato chips (empty calories)
- NPS data: when people quit Facebook and Instagram, they went from +35 to -35 NPS
- *"People are proud to tell you they're not on Instagram. They're not missing the party."*
- *"It's the biggest unexplored opportunity there is on the internet."*

### The Cocktail Party Metaphor
> *"We know it when we see a great cocktail party. You feel it. You're like, 'Oh, I'm so glad I'm here.' It moves from obligatory to almost like greed — I love this party, I love the people I'm meeting."*

- Napster: connected everyone, the great "lead" was a music file
- Friendster, Facebook, LinkedIn: better lead generation — social productivity
- Before: go to Google or Craigslist for a lead — lots of noise to signal
- After: get on Friendster and find a date — actually a good lead
- Zynga's move: *"I'm going to go to the cocktail party. Everyone's hanging out on social networks. I'm going to drop games in the middle and give them a new dimension to network."*
- Today: *"We're all hanging out on our Claude, on our GPT, but there's no cocktail party."*

### The Challenge to Founders
> *"Figure out how to make it rowdy. Figure out how to make that cocktail party social and socially productive and you will probably find gold there."*

### The Latent Demand Test
- In 2007: video gaming was a $23B business, but Mark didn't know anyone who played games
- Gaming was not even a top 10 activity on the web
- Mark believed: adults want to give themselves permission to play, but it's too hard
- Made it: free, a game you already know, three clicks and you're in, 5-15 minutes of your time
- *"We don't go to the comic book store, but if the comics were in the newspaper, we'd read them."*
- Result: gaming is now a $280B industry

### The Invest, Express, Connect Framework
Zynga's games worked because they let players:
- **Invest**: Do something creative (feel creative, not just be creative — like Midjourney today)
- **Express**: Be seen as creative by others
- **Connect**: Do it with friends, make new friends, co-op play, gifting

### The ASN Metric (Active Social Network) — Easter Egg
- Zynga built a metric nobody else uses: how many round trips did you have with another player?
  - You took a turn → they took a turn back
  - You gifted them → they gifted you back
- **0 to 1 ASN**: 80% chance of seeing you again next month
- **4 ASN**: 80% chance of seeing you 22 out of the next 30 days
- *"If you saw positive feedback loops — someone likes your photos, comments — that's probably the biggest dopamine hit we get."*

### Day 365 Retention
> *"I believe that the most valuable companies in the world, statistically, have the highest day 365 retention."*

- Zynga may have been the only consumer company tracking D365 retention
- You can't wait till day 365 — find early indicators
- High D1 and D30 correlate with D365
- But you can have high D30 and zero D365 — that's most products
- Viral-based companies are sinking speedboats: trying to drive faster than they're sinking
- *"You can either go faster, bail water out faster, or plug the hole in the boat — or build a better boat that doesn't have a hole."*

## 📦 Distribution in the AI Era

### Is AI a New Platform?
> *"AI is not yet a new platform. It is an important technology."*

- Not a hardware platform at the consumer side yet
- Not an interface platform yet (like Windows, browser, social network, or mobile)
- *"We're kind of halfway to a platform. We are in a point of discovery."*
- Average app installs per user per month: **zero**
- 40,000 new games launched last year in the app store → zero became top 10 hits

### The Distribution Reality
> *"Distribution has to be part of your product and part of baked into the strategy deeply and proven from the beginning. If you're just building this product and hoping they will come, that's a hope strategy, not a belief strategy."*

- It'll be 100x more expensive to try to get the same things in the market in a year
- Once there is distribution, there'll be a flood of competition
- When distribution is this broken, it's even more core to your product and strategy

### The Proumer Path
- Many more successful companies find a proumer approach
- Go after power users, whales — people who care enough to find it and spend money on it
- Sustain a business long before you can get out to consumer

### The Free Tokens Thesis (Gary Tan's Idea)
> *"If we get in the mindset of free tokens — if the amount of tokens we're buying today will basically be free in a year or two — and today we start reimagining consumer services based on free tokens, that's such an interesting innovation zone."*

- Not like dot-com (price of pig ears wasn't going to come down)
- But the price of tokens WILL come down
- Games that have to hit AI are hitting token limits — getting around that today might unlock really interesting innovation

### The Agentic Travel Agent — Easter Egg
> *"I think there should be an agentic travel agent. The most valuable part is when you're in the middle of a trip, be ready to rebook your flight and be on top of your travel logistics as they're happening."*

- Latent demand: we all want a 24/7 travel agent that knows our context
- Hasn't existed because travel agents couldn't make a living (not enough commissions)
- If baked into OpenAI or Claude, gives a clear reason for value beyond generic AI chat

### The Social Membrane
- Agent in the middle brokering social relationships
- Agent knows your context and my context
- Intelligent membrane of trust that dynamically changes based on attention level
- Could broker social scheduling without hurting feelings — knows you want to see me but also know you want to be with your kids Saturday

## 👥 Management & Leadership Principles

### Make Everyone a CEO
> *"All of management is just how do we get people to do the right thing when we're not in the room."*

- Give people a hill to take with operating control and degrees of freedom
- They give you a plan and budget, then do whatever they want within that
- Result: they don't come back with questions
- *"I'm a team player as long as I'm running the team."*
- Hire frustrated expert witnesses — people who think they have the right answers and have pent-up demand to prove it
- *"The best CEO is like the best player at the position — doing the thing they're great at, not wasting time on management."*

### Stay Close to the Metal
> *"The people in the trenches are usually closest to the data, closest to probably the right answer, but also usually furthest from making the decision."*

- Expert witness syndrome: you plead for what you passionately believe is right, get excused, they make a decision, you deal with the consequences
- The best product CEOs are in the minutia of the details
- Steve Jobs insisted on picking out carpeting in conference rooms
- Discord founders realized they were outsourcing the most important product decisions (UX) to their least experienced people → inverted the pyramid
- *"Micromanagement is beautiful. You should micromanage as long as you can."*
- Zynga standup: 50 employees, 2-hour call, every name in a spreadsheet, what they did yesterday, what they'll do today

### The Teaching Hospital / Tech Assistant
> *"How do you pass the vampire blood of you to other people? How do you spread that burning mission and passion?"*

- Put as many people as you can in the room while you're doing product management meetings
- Grab someone from the ranks, make them your tech assistant — have them follow you for 6-12 months
- Make them the mini-me: the person who is you in your 20s, the expert witness, a little bit of a know-it-all
- Andy Jassy at Amazon: everyone on the C-staff used to be someone who had been Bezos's tech assistant

### The Number One Job of a CEO
> *"If I get to pick one thing you do as CEO, I'm going to pick that you're right."*

- Being in the right body of water matters more than having the right boat
- A great boat in a dead lake bed isn't going anywhere
- *"I'll take misfits who are right."*
- Look for: are they right? That's the best resume.

## 🧒 Parenting in the AI Era

### Meeting Kids Where They Are
- Five kids, each a completely different version of a human
- Special needs son, one-year-old with a gene mutation, 15-year-old twins who are completely divergent from each other
- *"Meet them where they are, but as human to human — not talking down to them as kids, not treating them as adults. Find the way to engage at their altitude."*
- Daddy Math during pandemic: taught fifth graders all the way through eighth grade math without knowing it

### What to Teach in the AI Era
> *"We're at the end of this hundred-year cycle of mass-produced education — factory produced for factory workers, then knowledge workers. Knowledge working is changing, but we're still teaching knowledge education."*

- *"I don't care if you go to college. What I care about is that you develop critical thinking and find a way to be useful to people in the world."*
- Teach them to ask better questions, not know more answers
- When an adult is trying to convince you of something: understand their agenda, their lived experience, how that impacted their point of view

### Generative vs. Consumptive
> *"How can you be generative in what you're doing and not consumptive? What can you do online or offline that is actually putting something new out in the world?"*

- Carmen (ADHD and dyslexia): created "Comfy Fancy" sweatshirt brand and "Neurosparkley" — a group for neurodiverse middle school kids
- Took what should be a problem and deficit and made it a way to connect and help other kids

### The Life Philosophies Document
- Keeps a running Google Doc of life philosophies for his older girls
- Anything he says to them as a quote, he writes up with stories
- Examples:
  - *"Nothing's personal. If you assume nothing's personal, you're probably right 19 out of 20 times. And the 20th time, you probably handled it right because of that."*
  - *"Don't be a victim. The world doesn't happen to you, it's happening around you. We're defined by how we react to the world, not by the events that happen to us."*
