*Lenny's Podcast*

- **Brian Chesky** — CEO & Co-founder of Airbnb

## 🏗️ The Product Management Question: What Actually Happened

### What Brian Said at Figma Config (That Caused the Stir)
- Spoke to a room of 5,000 designers
- Someone tweeted he "eliminated product management"
- **Designers audibly cheered** — Brian's key observation: *"If I said I eliminated engineering, no one would have cheered. It was specifically that function."*
- This reaction itself is a signal worth examining

### What Actually Changed (3 Specific Moves)

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Combined PM + Product Marketing** | Inbound product development responsibilities merged with outbound/marketing responsibilities |
| **Off-boarded program management** | Many PMs were actually doing program management — reassigned to actual program managers (high-status role at Airbnb) |
| **Smaller, more senior groups** | Fewer junior people; everyone must understand how to talk about the product |

### The Core Philosophy
> *"You can't build a product unless you know how to talk about the product. You can't be an expert in making the product unless you're also an expert in marketing it."*

- Classic failure mode: ship a product, it doesn't work, conclude "we tried that"
- But was it a bad product? Bad strategy? Or **bad distribution**?
- *"If you build a great product and no one knows about it, did you even build a product?"*

### Why Designers Cheered
- Designers in Silicon Valley are deeply frustrated
- Brian's diagnosis: designers are treated as a **service organization** — "catching things before they go out the door" rather than driving product
- Many heads of design are actually **"design administrators"** running a service org
- This is bad for everyone — designers, PMs, and engineers all want to build great products

## 📉 The Decay Arc: How Companies Slow Down

Brian describes a universal pattern he observed at Airbnb (2015–2019):

### Stage-by-Stage Breakdown

1. **Technical divergence** — teams run on slightly different stacks, accumulate technical debt
2. **Dependency bottlenecks** — shared platforms (payments, etc.) become backed up like a deli queue
3. **Internal empire building** — teams stop waiting and build their own versions of shared functions (marketing, data, etc.)
4. **Division formation** — teams become separate mini-companies with their own interests
5. **Advocacy & politics** — resources go to whoever builds the best relationships, not best work
6. **Bureaucracy** — meetings about meetings, unclear accountability
7. **Complacency** — *"what I do doesn't matter"* mentality sets in
8. **Invisibility** — company does 10 marketing efforts, customers notice none; ships hundreds of features, customers can't name one

### The Engineering-Marketing Disconnect
> *"Marketing are the waiters, engineers are the chefs — and the chefs yell at the waiters when they come in the kitchen."*

- Waiters talk to customers all day and know how to sell
- Brian's heuristic: **the health of an organization = how close engineering and marketing are**

## 🔄 The Delegation Paradox

### The Founder's Journey (Almost Universal)
1. Founder runs everything, in the details
2. Encouraged to delegate and empower
3. Teams get more autonomy → optimization work, small thinking, bureaucracy
4. Founder realizes they need to take back control
5. Repeat

### Brian's Specific Experience
- The less involved he was → more spin, less clear goals, less advocacy, fewer resources, **slower movement**
- Teams assumed slowness was caused by over-involvement → asked for more autonomy
- Brian gave more autonomy → things got even slower
- *"They wanted to move faster, so I empowered them and they moved slower"*
- By 2019: spending **$1B on AdWords**, not investing in brand, heavy AB testing, product felt unchanged for 4 years

### The AB Testing Problem
Brian's nuanced view:
- Not anti-AB testing, but anti-**hypothesis-free** AB testing
- *"If we don't have a hypothesis and A is better than B, we're stuck with B forever"*
- 10 teams AB testing simultaneously = designing a house by AB testing the sofa without considering its relationship to the end tables, lamps, carpet, TV, and the whole house
- **Cohesive system thinking** vs. isolated metric optimization

## 💡 The Two People Who Changed Everything

### 1. Hiroki Asai (Pre-Pandemic)
- Former Creative Director at Apple, worked directly with Steve Jobs
- Ran **all of Apple's marketing communications** — including designing the app itself
- Key insight: at Apple, **marketing was the governing factor** that organized everything
- Every brand touchpoint flowed through marketing → created coherence
- Now at Airbnb: product design, product marketing, design, and marketing all report to him

### 2. Jony Ive
- Former Chief Design Officer at Apple
- Described Steve Jobs's way of running Apple (1998–2011)
- Key insight: **a design leader's job is managing the design first, people second**
- *"I could never imagine Jony Ive just being a manager of people — he was designing the work with the team"*
- You cannot give people development if you're not in the details of their work

## 🌊 The Pandemic as Catalyst

### The October 2019 Walk
- Brian walks with Joe Gebbia and Nathan Blecharczyk
- Shares a "dream": he left the company 10 years ago, was asked to come back, was **horrified** at what he found:
  - Amazing culture and mission ✅
  - Brand people loved ✅
  - Lost design roots ❌
  - Not investing long-term ❌
  - Obsessing over metrics ❌
  - No cohesive direction ❌
  - Great people leaving ❌
  - Costs rising, growth slowing ❌
- This was **exactly what was happening in reality**

### The Near-Death Experience
- Pandemic: **lost 80% of business in 8 weeks**
- *"Like your life flashing before your eyes — you have clarity"*
- Crisis gave Brian permission to do what he'd been wanting to do
- Before crisis: people said he was too involved. During crisis: *"What do we do? We need you more involved."*

## 🔧 The Transformation: Specific Changes Made

### Structural Changes

| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Written inventory** | Everything being worked on had to be documented in a Google Sheet — many couldn't even do it |
| **Radical prioritization** | Cut to ~20% of projects. *"Three teams should do one thing, not one team do three things"* |
| **Removed management layers** | As few layers as possible between Brian and individual contributors |
| **Functional model** | Eliminated divisions (flights, homes, experiences, China, etc.) → back to design, engineering, product marketing, marketing, comms, sales, ops |
| **Fewer, more senior people** | Fewer than **7,000 employees** (vs. Uber's ~30,000) |
| **Expert leaders only** | Every executive must be an expert in their functional domain — no pure people managers |
| **CEO review schedule** | Brian reviews all product and marketing on cadences: weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, every 8 weeks, or every 12 weeks |
| **Green/yellow/red scoring** | Head program manager scores all projects on track status |
| **Two launches per year** | May and November/October — nothing ships unless it's on the road map |
| **Rolling 2-year road map** | Updated every 6 months; near-term doesn't change, far-term evolves |

### The Review System as Organizational Infrastructure
> *"Imagine we're a car company and I see the car prototype every week. I notice something about the tire is off. I can identify the individual person who was blocked."*

- Reviews replace the need for mandated office attendance — Brian can track work quality remotely
- Reviews replace the need for political influence — if something isn't happening, Brian stops the meeting and asks why
- Reviews create accountability without bureaucracy
- Every week: see the equivalent of a **semi-assembly of the entire new product**

### Marketing & Creative Changes
- Built **in-house creative agency** — no more Wieden+Kennedy or Saatchi
- Eliminated **UX writing as a separate function** — merged with marketing writing
  - *"The emails, the app, the ads should all be one voice"*
  - Writing reports to a unified writing function, not to design
- Shifted from performance marketing to **product marketing + brand**

### Performance Marketing Philosophy
Joe Gebbia's metaphor:
- **Performance marketing = laser** (lights up a corner of a room)
- **Brand marketing = chandelier** (lights up the whole room)
- *"You don't want to use a bunch of lasers to light up an entire room"*
- Performance marketing doesn't create **accumulating advantages** — it's not an investment
- Exception: if you have very high ROI (like Booking.com), you can build an arbitrage business

## 📅 The Rolling Road Map System

- **No annual planning cycles** — planning is just a budgeting exercise now
- **Rolling 2-year road map** updated every 6 months
- Two releases per year: May and November
- *"Each release is a chapter of a story, or an episode of a TV series"*
- Think of the company as a **5–10 year story**
- Reserve resources for unexpected pivots (e.g., housed **120,000 Ukrainian refugees** quickly)
- Story-first development: *"One of the first things we do on a launch is figure out what the story is — the story often dictates the product"*
- Goal: *"A company where 1,000 people work, but it looks like 10 people did it"*

## 🚀 The Winter Release: Guest Favorites

### The Core Problem Airbnb Solved
- Every Airbnb home is unique → **you don't always know what you're going to get**
- Hotels: less special, but **you know exactly what you'll get**
- Reliability = Airbnb's Achilles heel
- Goal: combine **Airbnb's uniqueness** with **hotel-level reliability**

### Guest Favorites
- Analyzed **370 million reviews** + millions of customer service tickets + host cancellation data
- Identified **top 2 million homes** = "Guest Favorites"
- Represents homes guests rate highest across all signals

### Other Launch Elements
- **Overhauled ratings & reviews system**
- **New listing tab** for hosts — described as "quite possibly one of the nicest things we've ever designed"
  - Old listing management was a "hodgepodge designed by different teams over many years"
  - Theory: *"If we put care in the design of our app, hosts will see that and put more care into hosting"*
- **AI-powered photo tour** — trained on 100 million photos, scans and organizes photos by room
- **New design aesthetic**: moving away from flat design toward color, texture, dimensionality
  - Brian's prediction: *"Flat design is over"*
  - 2000s = skeuomorphism → 2010s = flat design (iOS 7) → now = dimensional, colorful, textured
  - Reason: more time on screens → want screens to replicate natural environment (light, texture)
  - AI enables more sophisticated interfaces

### Why This Required the New Way of Working
- Guest Favorites required guest team + host team + communications + market education working together
- Old divisional structure (separate guest team, separate host team) would have made this impossible
- Now: **one design team, one engineering team** — fungible across guest and host problems

## 🎯 Brian's Checklist for Founders

1. **CEO = Chief Product Officer** — if you're a product/tech company, the CEO should own product
2. **Everyone interconnected** — every PM should know what everyone else is doing; no silos unless truly independent
3. **Leaders must be domain experts** — no pure people managers anywhere in the company
4. **Fewer people** — grow slowly; five teams do one thing rather than one team do five things
5. **Do launches** — you can ship continuously but package it and tell a story
6. **Data + research + intuition** — *"You can't delegate understanding"* (Charles Eames)
7. **Engineering and design report to founder/product person** — not design under product
8. **PM responsibility includes distribution** — understanding customers and telling the story
9. **PMs should be art + science** — not purely technical if working with non-technical functions
10. **Engineering and marketing interconnected** — joined at the hip
11. **Minimal layers** — CEO should treat directs-of-directs as dotted-line reports
12. **Everyone rows in the same direction** — *"Otherwise, why are you all in the same company?"*

## 🔢 The "Add a Zero" Philosophy

### What It Actually Means
- Not literally demanding 10x results
- The **exercise** of imagining 10x forces different thinking about the problem
- *"You can't do the current process to achieve 10x — you have to think differently"*
- Thinking differently requires **deeply understanding** the problem
- Deep understanding requires **breaking it into first principles**

### Setting Pace Through Decisiveness
> *"If you want to improve the speed of a company, make faster decisions."*

- Pace is governed not by how hard people work but **how decisive they are**
- Bias for action: don't "circle back next week" — stay in the meeting until it's done
- *"You end up getting three months of work done in that period"*

### The John Wooden Story
- Winningest college basketball coach in history
- Secret: *"I just asked my players to do their very best"*
- The implicit thing: **he saw potential in people they didn't see in themselves**
- Leader's role: see potential in people beyond what they show you
- Fixed mindset: "what I'm doing isn't good enough" → demoralized
- Growth mindset: "the more Brian pushes, the more he believes in me" → motivated
- Andy Grove's framework: **competency × motivation** — a crisis reveals true capability

## ⚖️ Burnout, Balance & Personal Life

### The Paradox of Being More Involved
> *"The more in the details I am, the more time I have on my hands."*

- First 1–2 years of transformation: **significantly more work** than the old way
- After turning the corner:
  - People row in the same direction without being told
  - Fewer conflicts
  - Less turnover
  - Went from **10 surprises, 9 bad → 10 surprises, 9 good**
  - No longer intervening in dysfunctional projects late (3x harder to fix)
  - Culture = what happens when you're not in the room

### Brian's Personal Practices
- **Exercise**: 20 min morning cardio (Peloton) + weights 3–4x/week + daily cardio
- **Diet**: Classic bodybuilding diet, 5–6 meals/day
- **Sleep**: Prioritizes getting enough
- **Work rhythm**: Every other weekend completely off; alternating weekends work intensely
- **Relationships**: Group chats + annual trips with high school friends AND college friends
  - *"If you don't travel with old friends, you only have old stories — you need new shared experiences"*
  - Stays at sister's apartment in NYC to see her
- **Creative outlets**: Drawing, reading

### The Reactive Trap for Founders
> *"A lot of founders spend their time reacting. People email them, and suddenly their email sets the agenda."*

- Old Brian: so busy reacting that friends assumed he was too busy to reach out → he never heard from them → got isolated
- Fix: **strategy-first scheduling** — what relationships do I need to execute this strategy?
- *"Say no to fake work — things that feel like work but don't move the ball"*
- The mortality frame: *"If your life were to end in a year, who would you have wanted to spend time with?"*
- Also applies to companies: if you only respond to employees who reach out, introverts get no attention

### The Harvard Happiness Study
- 85-year study on human happiness
- Single answer: **healthy relationships**
- Brian's three pillars: **health + meaningful work + relationships**

- Picasso: *"It took me 4 years to learn to paint like Raphael, but a lifetime to learn to paint like a child"*

## 🎨 The Fun Fact: Brian's Origin Story as an Artist

### The Full Arc
- Age 5: Obsessed with Norman Rockwell at the museum, tried to reproduce his paintings
- Elementary school: Asked Santa for **poorly designed toys so he could redesign them**
- Age 8–9: Discovered architectural drawing at a friend's house → got into architectural and landscape design
- Got Disney annual reports by having his dad buy Disney stock → designed theme parks and communities
- Played **ice hockey** at a military sports academy — thought he'd play college hockey
- Same art teacher grades 8–11 (not good — no diversification)
- Transferred to public high school junior year → met **Ms. Williams**, who told his mom: *"Don't worry, he's going to be a famous artist one day"*
- Won national art competition → artwork displayed in the **Rotunda Gallery**
- Scholarship to **RISD** (Rhode Island School of Design)
- At RISD: felt born 100 years too late for painting/drawing (photography already replacing those skills)
- Discovered **industrial design** — *"the design of everything from a toothbrush to a spaceship"*

### Why Industrial Design Was the Perfect Foundation
- Had to work with mechanical and electrical engineers
- Had to understand manufacturing
- **Accountable to sales** — unlike architecture, if your product doesn't sell, you're on the hook
- Forced understanding of marketing and strategy
- Met **Joe Gebbia** at RISD — day of graduation: *"Brian, I think we're going to start a company together one day"*

### The Airbnb Origin Moment
- Moved to LA, worked as industrial designer for 2 years
- Got a package in the mail from Joe: a seat cushion with a handle + letter saying *"come to San Francisco"*
- This was 2007 — YouTube had just launched, Brian discovered Steve Jobs keynotes for the first time
- Quit his job, packed everything in an old Honda Civic, drove to SF
- Rent was $1,150 — didn't have enough money
- Design conference came to SF, all hotels sold out
- *"What if we turn our house into a bed and breakfast?"*
- Joe had 3 air mattresses → **Air Bed and Breakfast** → Airbnb

### The Deeper Point
> *"There's not a lot of designers or artists running Fortune 500 companies. And I think that intuition, imagination, design curiosity — we need more of that."*

- Companies are like bodies cut off at the head, disconnected from their heart
- Einstein played violin — being whole and well-rounded matters
- Industrial design background explains everything: the obsession with cohesion, aesthetics, user experience, and the integration of art and engineering
