## 🏗️ The Three W's: Career Growth Framework for PMs

### The Core Insight
- Working on a cool product area does NOT guarantee career growth
- Some PMs whose products did really well didn't grow
- Some PMs whose products didn't have huge impact grew rapidly
- The difference: **how they operated across three dimensions**

### The Three W's

| W | What It Means | Key Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **What you produce** | Outputs and outcomes | Execution, shipping, artifacts |
| **What you bring to the table** | Impact on impact | Quality of your contributions |
| **What's your operating model** | How you work with others | Communication, collaboration, decisions |

> *"The really strong product managers who were also growing in their careers did some things differently. The ones who rise are performing well on all three axes."*

---

## 💡 W1: What You Produce

### The Output → Outcome → Direction Progression

**Outputs** (early career focus):
- Launching a product
- Running and analyzing an experiment
- Contributing to a go-to-market strategy
- Preparing a brief for a summit
- Creating a first draft of a leadership review slide
- Ranking content sources by ease of acquisition
- *"Something which is very tangibly defined, which doesn't take too much time to visualize and strategize over"*

**Outcomes** (mid-career):
- Product areas and goals you can own or collaborate on
- Controlling which outputs are necessary

**Direction/Leadership** (senior):
- Figuring out which outcomes are necessary
- Setting strategic direction

### The Critical Mistake
> *"The mistake I see a lot of product managers make is they start operating in either output or outcomes. When you are transitioning to outcomes, it's very important that you continue to still hone your craft on outputs."*

- Don't abandon outputs when you move up
- Even at the most senior levels: don't forget your IC roots
- Going back to IC work gives you credibility with the team

### The Mini CEO Trap
- Folklore around product management can confuse people
- Early in career: you haven't earned the right to inform strategy and vision
- People need to trust you first — that comes from executing well
- *"Even if you are senior and starting with a new team or company, you need to have that mindset"*

### What Good Output Looks Like
- Volunteer and own first drafts of things leaders are blocked on
- Find the small things that make an impact AND contribute to your learning
- Be useful in doing small things vs. trying to be the "mini CEO"
- Example: PM who ranked content sources by ease of acquisition → made everyone's life simpler → fed into overall strategy

---

## 💡 W2: What You Bring to the Table

### The Core Concept: Impact on Impact
> *"You have to show that yes, you were a useful contributor to having that particular impact."*

- Your product area being successful doesn't automatically guarantee your growth
- You must demonstrate YOUR contribution to that success

### The Artifact Test
When PMs complain they're not growing despite working on impactful areas, Vikrama asks:
- *"Can you show me your last PRD?"*
- *"Can you show me the last product note that you sent?"*
- *"Can you show me the product strategy doc?"*
- *"Can you show me the brief that you sent to the design team on the problems and the ranking of those problems?"*

Usually something is missing.

### What "Bringing to the Table" Looks Like
- PRD quality
- Draft notes that circulate to care teams and marketing teams
- Deriving from strategy (vs. constantly pushing back on strategy)
- Drafting north stars
- Working on experiments, data, metrics
- Pre-sprint planning documents
- Jira storyboards with actual content (not just titles)

### The Wrong vs. Right Approach
**Wrong**: Bringing arguments, debates, pushbacks
**Right**: Channelizing questions, inputs, and direction → converting into strategic choices that shape discussions and direction

> *"Be the latter and you will rise faster in your career."*

### The Four Pillars You Must Demonstrate
1. Data and metrics
2. Design and research
3. Technology skills
4. Strategy

*"Product managers constantly are evaluated on this when you interview, but you also have to demonstrate these on the job."*

---

## 💡 W3: What's Your Operating Model

### The Three Tenets of Working Well with Others

> *"These three tenets are easy to say, but they become very, very hard to embody and display on a day-to-day basis."*

1. **Raise difficult issues without being difficult to work with**
2. **Bring out important topics without drawing importance to yourself**
3. **Be in charge of getting decisions made, not making all the decisions yourself**

### On Pushback: How to Do It Well
- Bring the tempo of the conversation from emotional space to logical space
- *"A lot of this is something that leaders need to ensure is happening, but product managers who are able to bring this tempo down will also do far better in their careers"*
- Don't be seen as an obstacle or hindrance
- Be seen as someone who adds value and advances the product forward

### The Operating Model Becomes Most Critical
- Most important when going from mid-senior to senior level
- Encompasses: communication, collaboration, organizational skills, community skills

## 🚫 What Impedes Career Growth: Three Mindset Traps

### Trap 1: Focusing on Things Outside Your Control

**Early career pattern**: Obsessed with feedback, focused on craft, output, personal growth

**Mid-senior pattern shift** (the problem):
- "Why is the organization not doing this for me?"
- "Why can't that stakeholder change this about themselves?"
- "Why don't I get to work on projects like this?"

> *"It is very, very important that you keep your focus no matter what stage of career you get into on what you can control."*

**The fix**: Go back to the three W's — there are years of work to do on data, technology, communication, collaboration, design, research, strategy, and community. Focus on those.

### Trap 2: Slowing Your Rate of Change

**Early career**: Rate of change is crazy — growing every 6 months, picking up new skills constantly

**Mid-senior problem**: 
- "Maybe I should not take on this product"
- "I don't know what it means for my career"
- Rate of change slows → rate of growth slows

**The fix**: Constantly check what you can do to increase your rate of change
- If you're a 4/5 on data within your org → benchmark against the best in the industry → your score drops → you see what to improve
- If you're a 4/5 on communication within PM → map yourself to other functions → score drops again → new growth areas emerge

> *"Keep your focus on rate of change and rate of growth will automatically take care of itself."*

### Trap 3: The Stories You Tell Yourself

**The problem**: The labels you give yourself become limiting

Examples:
- "I'm a high agency PM" → becomes an excuse for being brash, cutting corners, being rude
- "I'm a hyper-collaborative PM" → becomes an excuse for being indecisive, moving slowly

> *"These stories are defining you at a basic level, which is then very hard to correct through frameworks and structures."*

**The fix**:
1. Figure out what stories you're telling yourself
2. Talk to people you trust to get their perspective
3. Correct those stories
4. *"The moment you are able to correct those stories, you may be back on the growth path again"*

**Vikrama's personal example**:
- Thought he was great at data → saw Crystal Widjaja's work at Gojek → immediately recalibrated
- Thought his communication and strategy skills were very good → worked with Dito and Sidu → challenged to see what he was missing
- Shifted to: *"I'm still in a learning phase"* — an enabling story
- Changed from "high agency PM" to "mindful agency person" — especially important working in Southeast Asian cultures

## 🎯 The Eight Axes of PM Skills

### The Four Core Technical Axes

**1. Data and Metrics**
- Level 0: Can't define basic metrics for a product, can't figure out if something is impacting orders/users/revenue
- Level 5 (Crystal Widjaja level): Could probably do a data startup of your own

**2. Design and Research**
- Level 0: Can identify problems from a user's perspective
- Level 5: Can define user problems AND tie them to business roles — the holistic view

**3. Technology**
- Level 0: Doesn't know what HTTP, API, or internet means
- Level 5: Can have deep technical debates, could probably write technology design documents

**4. Strategy**
- Not corporate strategy, business strategy, or pricing strategy
- **Product strategy**: Once someone defines the mountain to climb, HOW do you climb it?
  - Which user segments to focus on?
  - What are their needs?
  - What's the right product for them?
  - In what order do we work on things?
- Level 0: Relies on everyone to tell you what to do
- Level 5: Can articulate a very coherent product strategy at a broad level

### The Four Operating Axes
5. **Communication**
6. **Collaboration**
7. **Organizational skills**
8. **Community** — *"Product manager is the all-community enabler in the team"*

> *"Community is the software aspect which ties everyone together towards a common mission of delivering an output."*

Especially important for remote/distributed teams.

## 🔄 Transitioning Into Product Management

### The Framework for Career Transitions
- Pick the skill axes you're weakest on based on your background
- **Coming from design/research background**: Focus on data or tech
- **Coming from data/tech background**: Focus on design and research
- This gives maximum leverage because it's the skill you haven't developed

### Real Examples from Gojek

**PM from growth/data background**:
- Given: Finding driver redesign (very design-focused product)
- Approach: Leveraged data skills to create PRD with incredible data on customer segments, then worked with designers on framework
- Result: *"A piece of art"* — product that shows what's possible when you combine backgrounds

**PM from research background**:
- Focus: Technology and data skills
- Needed: Engineers to confirm she could work with them strongly
- Result: Turned a heated question on a feature into a full-blown solution with designs, trade-offs, and a leadership question on direction

### Watch-Outs for Career Transitions
- Doesn't work out always — some PMs pick things up faster than others
- Harder if you're already senior in a function when transitioning
- *"Sometimes it's okay to go a little slow when you're transitioning so that you're able to go faster later"*
- Requires a strong product leader working with you to shape those skills

## 🤔 What Is Product Management? (The Ambiguity Problem)

### The Reality
- Still figuring out the exact definition after several decades
- Even the strongest definitions are philosophical, not concrete
- Different companies, different teams, different divisions define it very differently
- Stakeholders don't know what to expect from PMs either

### Vikrama's Answer to Stakeholders
> *"If this is something which is blocking the progress on the product, then yes, the product manager should work on that."*

### The PM as the Connective Tissue
- Four circles: strategy, technology, design, data
- PM circle encircles all four
- *"You are the only discipline which is the co-collaborator for all of these disciplines"*
- You always collaborate and pair with someone else — but you're the only one pairing with everyone
- This gives you unique insight no one else on the team has

### The Key Test
> *"When you are added to the team, you must drive the team's overall contribution, overall energy and overall output up and not down."*

## 💬 Contrarian Views

### 1. Intent Is Not Enough
- Common belief: as long as your intent is right, even if words don't land, it'll work out
- Vikrama's view: *"It's not enough. Your actions, your behavior, and the way you communicate — that also has to communicate and show who you are."*
- Resonates more with people starting their careers than with his age group

### 2. Hours Still Matter
> *"You still need to put in the effort and our number of hours is effort."*

- Becoming politically incorrect to say
- But: you have to spend time, effort, and energy into growing
- Not just about company growth — it's about your own growth
- The debate between workaholism and not being serious about growth is losing the nuance

## 📋 Feedback: How to Actually Use It

### The Challenge
- Product management is ambiguous → stakeholders give feedback on many dimensions, some not even important
- You have to shape feedback into: is this an area I'm targeting for growth?

### The Strategy
1. **Be genuinely open to feedback** — easy to say, hard to actually do
2. **Pick one focus area** — you can't improve on everything simultaneously
3. **Fix the most negative things first** — highest leverage
4. **Don't try to improve data, design, strategy, and technology all at once** — that's overwhelming

### Time Horizons for Different Skills
- **Soft skills** (communication, collaboration, community): Work on these your entire life — you'll never reach 5/5
- **Technical skills** (data, tech): More defined progression, clearer milestones
- **Starting out**: Pick one from data/tech AND one from design/research
